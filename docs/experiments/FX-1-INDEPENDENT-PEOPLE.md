# FX-1: the independent people, in plain words (2026-10-09)

Why they exist: everything in FX-1 (the formulas, the checker, the fixtures, the elements and the thresholds) was written by the GS side. That is the weakness the registration admits (`prereg\FX-1.md` section on threats). The independent people do not make it independent; they reduce the bias on the three places where it hurts most. Time estimates are guesses to be adjusted. Nobody should be paid with something that depends on the result.

| Role | What they do | What they must NOT see before | Estimated time | Who can do it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Fixture author** (writes the briefs and the real legacy code the runs start from) | Writes five invented project briefs (a web API, a CLI, a data pipeline, game logic, a tool server) as a product owner would, in plain language, with acceptance criteria; and for migration (path C) chooses or writes small legacy-style projects. | The formulas, the checker, the 12 elements, GS vocabulary. | about 8 to 12 hours | A working engineer or product owner who has not read GS. Not Gabriel (partner), not Esteban (client). |
| **2. Cold brief reader** | Reads the formulas as a first-time user, in order, and writes down what is unclear, what they would skip, and what they would misread, before any run. | The checker and the results. | about 2 to 3 hours | Any engineer unfamiliar with GS; a teacher or a colleague of Gabriel's. |
| **3. Control author** | Builds the known-good and the broken projects (each element removed, each element present but broken) WITHOUT reading the checker's patterns, so the checker is tested against someone else's idea of "broken". | The checker's source and its own controls. | about 8 to 10 hours | A verification-minded engineer (testing, CI, security tooling). This is also the best candidate for the outside review of `gs-check`. |
| **4. Human auditor** | Judges a held-out sample of runs (24 runs, 288 element judgments) by looking at the project and the probe log, blind to which formula or model produced it, and records whether the checker was right. | Which cell each run belongs to; the checker's patterns (they see the purpose of each element and the probe log). | about 10 to 14 hours | A senior engineer with no stake in GS. |

Conditions for independence (written down before they start): no commercial tie to PragmaWorks, a declared conflict-of-interest statement, they work from a written brief rather than a conversation with you, their output is kept as they wrote it (only typos are fixed), and they are free to say the design is bad. Credit: acknowledgement by name if they agree; co-authorship only by the standard authorship criteria, decided later and not promised.

Where to find them: Gabriel's contacts (for the fixture author and the cold reader, excluding people in his company who know GS), the open-science and empirical software engineering communities listed in `C:\workspace\PragmaWorks\soma\docs\research-partners-and-resources-2026-10-03.md` (for the control author and the auditor), and a small honorarium through a freelance marketplace if no volunteer appears. Ask for the smallest piece first (the cold brief reader, 2 to 3 hours): it tests whether the person is a good fit.

What you do: choose the person for role 2 this week, send the formulas and the one-paragraph request, and read what comes back before anything is frozen. The rest can wait for the diagnostic stage (FX-0).
