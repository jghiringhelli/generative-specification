## 1. Question, and what it does and does not answer

SDX-1 asks whether the substrate adds state-dependent correctness over ten changes on a project small enough for a fresh session to read whole. It cannot reach the regime where the substrate is claimed to matter most (SDX-1 section 10a (a)), and it says nothing about cost over time. SDX-8 asks the question a buyer and a skeptic both ask: **the substrate costs something to set up and keep true; as the repository grows, does that overhead pay off, and from which change on?**

The part of the METR-type objection it addresses: "a heavy process adds overhead and the AI speed-up is lost as work accumulates". The studies attribute the loss to review burden, accumulating complexity and warnings, and long-horizon erosion (dissection note sections 1 and 4). SDX-8 tests the durable-cost part: does an enforced substrate keep the marginal cost of an accepted change from rising with repository size, and does cumulative cost, with its own overhead inside, fall below the comparators at a registered change?

What it does NOT answer, and the registration says so in its wording: METR's own finding (experienced humans, mature familiar repositories, tacit knowledge, review and prompting time) is untouched: this is model-only, on an invented domain, and GS cannot supply knowledge nobody wrote down (mechanisms M8, M12, M13 of the dissection note). Not a head-start claim. Not a claim that the substrate was cheap to write for a human (human authoring hours are reported, view V3). Not a claim about repositories beyond about 18 kLOC (regime (a1) of section 10a).

Honest prior, written before any data (protocol guard f; **DRAFT by the assistant, JC must confirm or overwrite before any data**): the substrate is slower than an expert prompt in the first ten changes (H8-c, a registered risk, not a hope); a net crossover, if any, is in the second half of the chain; "no crossover within 30 changes" is a live outcome with probability near 40% as a statement about the world, and **a much higher probability as a statement about what the registered procedure will report** (section 2a, "Power": at the central prior and planning n the procedure reports a crossover with probability of order 10%). Evidence for: SX (a map collapses search cost, tier C), SlopCodeBench and the Cursor panel study (cost and complexity rise with size), DORA. Evidence against: Gloaguen and Khatri (context files add more than 20% cost without success gains at the sizes they test), the CodeSeeker obsolescence note (frontier models navigate without a map at about 4x cost, which cuts both ways), no study of a heavy substrate's net effect.

Intuition in falsifiable form: "On a mid-tier coding agent extending a repository from about 3 to about 18 kLOC over 30 scripted changes, the substrate's growth of marginal cost per accepted change is at least 15% lower than under an expert prompt, with or without a generic must-pass gate, and its cumulative net cost per accepted change falls at least 15% below both by change 30." One falsifier list is used everywhere in this file: **EQUIVALENT, COSTLIER or CHEAPER-SMALL on a co-primary contradicts this version of the intuition (REFUTED for this version); INCONCLUSIVE leaves it open (state the n that would resolve it).** Versions nothing could contradict ("it pays off eventually") are not the hypothesis; the horizon is registered.

## 2. Hypotheses, readouts, falsifiers, controls

### 2.1 Readouts, defined once

Unit: one chain = CR0 plus 30 scripted changes in a fresh session each, only the repository carrying state (the SDX-1 rule), run by the same harness extended per section 4. The identical task prompt of every arm ends with "commit your work before ending the session", so each arm's own hooks execute on the agent's commits; harness snapshot commits go to a separate ref with hooks disabled and never run any arm's gate. (SDX-1 section 3 has the harness commit; if SDX-1's critics change that, the point is routed to SDX-1's adjudication, section 13.)

**Cost.** Cost of change k = all model spend of every session and every attempt for that change (retries per the SDX-1 retry rule, gate-failure loops, upkeep of the arm's own artifacts), dollars from a price table registered at freeze (input, output, cache creation, cache read; per model snapshot) so that vendor price changes cannot move the result; tokens (same four categories) and agent wall-clock are second and third currencies, reported with the same analysis, never mixed. **Cache-neutral sensitivity table:** the same analysis with cache reads charged at the uncached input rate (a stable, large substrate preamble is read largely at the cache rate, which would discount exactly the reading overhead the comparison is meant to charge). A cost verdict row applies only if the dollar result and the cache-neutral result fall in the same class family; otherwise the qualifier Q5 applies ("currency-dependent: no cost claim"). Three cost views, always reported together:
- **V1 (registered; the verdict view):** all in-chain spend plus **Z_project**, charged once per chain as if every chain were a new project. Z_project = the **median model spend of three registered regeneration runs**, executed by the harness at freeze, of a fixed generator script and model that rebuilds the project-specific files of each arm from the base spec (for A4: the base-spec-derived specs of its initial substrate; for the other arms zero); the file-by-file classification of A4's initial substrate into project-specific (charged) and generic (not charged) is registered at freeze and reviewed by the independent reviewer. The reusable generic assets (A4's protocol, hooks and templates, A5's prompt, A5g's hook) are not dollars in V1 and are reported in author hours.
- **V2 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 without Z_project.
- **V3 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 plus all authoring hours of every arm, and the human review hours of project-specific files, priced at the BACKLOG rate of $75 per hour, charged once per chain (the one-project scenario; expected to be adverse to the substrate and reported so a reader can see how adverse). Human hours stay out of V1 because they are not model dollars and A5's authoring hours would otherwise need the same treatment.

**Two acceptance flags per change** (sealed, arm-blind, deterministic; no judge):
- **A3_k, lag-3 acceptance (used for the growth readout and for R):** (1) at the snapshot after change k at most one of the change's fresh probes fails; (2) at the snapshot after change min(k+3, 30) at most one of that change's probes that are still live (not retired or inverted by the manifest) fails. A change that introduces no fresh probes (change 18, a behaviour-preserving restructure) is accepted iff at most one live probe of all earlier changes fails at k and at min(k+3, 30). Every change is thus judged with the same look-ahead (up to three later changes), so no window is exposed to more erosion than another.
- **A∞_k, final durability (used only in the secondary R∞):** A3_k and (3) at the final snapshot at most one of the change's live probes fails and no held-out open-field probe tagged to change k fails (an escaped defect cancels the acceptance of the change whose behaviour it tests).
A chain whose build fails on two consecutive changes is **collapsed**: later changes count as not accepted and sessions keep running and costing (ITT); completers-only is the sensitivity. Deviation from the dissection note, stated: its third criterion ("no corrective follow-up change") is dropped because the chain is scripted and a harness-written repair request would have to describe the failing behaviour and leak the oracle; a regression instead cancels the acceptance without any repair session, and the arm pays for erosion through lost verified output.

**V3_i(k)** = number of changes among 1..k with A3 set; **V∞_i(k)** likewise with A∞. **C_i(k)** = cumulative cost through change k (CR0 included, view V1 unless stated).

- **Marginal cost per accepted change in a window**, windows W1 = changes 1..10 and **W3 = changes 18..27** (both windows have the full three-change look-ahead inside the chain; changes 28 to 30 enter R and the secondary readouts only): m_i(w) = (cost of the sessions of the changes in w, CR0 and Z excluded) / max(number of A3 changes in w, 2). The denominator floor is 2 (not 0.5: a collapsed chain would otherwise add ln 16 to the log growth ratio and one chain could move an arm mean by nearly the SESOI); collapse rates are reported as a co-outcome and rows (R2) to (R4) require the result to hold with and without collapsed chains. Sensitivities: floor 0.5, chains with fewer than 2 accepted excluded.
- **Growth ratio** g_i = m_i(W3) / m_i(W1). **Slope estimator** b_i = ordinary-least-squares slope, per chain, of log(cost of change k) on log(repository size at the start of change k, kLOC) over changes k = 1..30 (all changes, ITT; no ratio of integers, so far less noisy). **Selection rule, registered now:** SDX-8-P computes the arm-pooled SD (no arm contrast, no arm label) of log g and of b over its 12 chains; the estimator with the smaller pooled SD and smaller upper 80% limit becomes the primary readout of H8-1 at freeze, the other a registered sensitivity. Both are reported either way.
- **Net cumulative cost per accepted change** R_i(k) = C_i(k) / max(V3_i(k), 0.5), view V1 (A3 flags, so R at any checkpoint uses at most three changes of look-ahead and R(30) uses the final snapshot only for changes 28 to 30). **R∞_i(30)** = C_i(30) / max(V∞_i(30), 0.5), the quality-charged view in which escaped defects cancel acceptances (secondary; qualifier Q6).
- **Verified throughput within a budget** VT_i(B) = V3_i(k_B), where k_B is the last change completed before cumulative cost exceeds B, B = median C(30) of the cheapest arm (secondary).

**Primary readouts:** H8-1 on the registered growth estimator (log g or b); H8-2 on log R at the registered checkpoints.

### 2.2 Classification of any contrast

Delta = mean of log X minus mean of log Y per chain (negative = X cheaper), 97.5% intervals for the two co-primaries (alpha 0.025 each, Bonferroni), 95% for secondary. **The class is decided by the BCa interval** (10,000 replicates, seed registered); the Monte Carlo permutation p-value (100,000, seed registered) is reported beside it and decides nothing; if the two disagree on the class the less favourable class is reported. BCa coverage is checked on pilot-shaped simulated data before freeze, with percentile-t as the registered fallback if coverage falls below 0.93. Interval [L, U], point estimate p, SESOI delta = ln(0.85) in magnitude (a 15% difference), provisional (justification at the freeze; it is fixed from the decision value of a saving, before the pilot, and the pilot may not move it). Classes are a **partition**:

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| CHEAPER-RELEVANT | U < 0 and p <= minus delta |
| CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED | U < 0, p > minus delta, L < minus delta |
| CHEAPER-SMALL | U < 0 and L >= minus delta (a measurable saving that is below the registered minimum worth having) |
| EQUIVALENT | the interval contains 0 and lies inside minus delta to plus delta (TOST at half the alpha per side) |
| COSTLIER | L > 0 (COSTLIER-RELEVANT if p >= delta) |
| INCONCLUSIVE | everything else |

**"Positive" means CHEAPER-RELEVANT or CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED, nothing else.** CHEAPER-SMALL is reported as a saving below relevance and licenses no claim that the overhead was "repaid". Order of weakness used when a claim needs the weaker of two contrasts: COSTLIER, EQUIVALENT, INCONCLUSIVE, CHEAPER-SMALL, CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED, CHEAPER-RELEVANT.

**Power.** The earlier SD grid (0.15 to 0.30) is withdrawn: with ten-change windows, binomial acceptance counts alone give an SD of log g near 0.26 (two windows, acceptance probability 0.75), and adding per-window cost variation of 0.25 gives about 0.4 to 0.5; the pilot's three chains per arm (8 degrees of freedom) cannot settle it. For 97.5% two-sided intervals, 80% power for a positive class at a true gap equal to delta (0.163): n per arm = 2 (3.08 x SD / 0.163)^2. SD 0.15 gives 16, 0.20 gives 29, 0.25 gives 45, 0.30 gives 64, **0.45 gives 145, 0.60 gives 257**. Planning n = 20 detects, at 80% power, a gap of about 0.97 SD: 0.19 at SD 0.20, **0.44 (a 36% saving) at SD 0.45**. The slope estimator and log R(30) are expected to have much smaller SD (per-chain slopes use all 30 changes; cumulative cost sums 30 sessions), which is why the selection rule of section 2.1 exists; the pilot reports both. **Probability that the registered procedure reports a crossover, computed at the central prior:** for log R(30) with central ratio 0.95 (|log| 0.051), SD 0.20 and n = 20 per arm, single-contrast power at the 97.5% level is about 0.08 (z = 2.24 against a standardized gap of 0.81), lower for the intersection-union over two comparators and lower again for an earlier checkpoint. **At the authors' own central prior, "no crossover within the horizon" would be reported with probability above 0.9, and it is consistent with every prior in this table: the modal registered outcome changes no decision.** Therefore, before freeze, the pilot-informed simulation of section 14 item 2 is mandatory, and if the simulated probability that the registered procedure reports a crossover (given the central prior and the approved n) is below 0.5, **the experiment is bought as an estimation study**: it reports c*_fit, the cost curves and intervals, and the rows for H8-2 are limited to INCONCLUSIVE-family statements, with JC's signed acceptance of that before any chain runs. n can be raised from the pooled SD (arms pooled, no contrast) and its upper 80% limit, by one blinded re-estimation at the interim look, only inside the approved budget cap; delta cannot be raised after the pilot.

### 2b. Controls and validity gates (guard a)

- **Growth-regime positive control (A0):** g(A0) at least 2.0 and repository size at change 30 at least 5 times size at CR0. If not, **H8-1 is not testable as designed and H8-2 is read as net cost without growth**: row R1 (a citable finding: "no cost-growth regime at this size on this fixture; the growth premise is not supported here"), not an invalid design for H8-2.
- **State positive control A0S** (antecedent texts and scripted replies restated in later change texts, only what each state-dependent probe needs, n = 10): A0S minus A0 on E1-SD at snapshot 30 at least 20 points, the SDX-1 rule on the longer chain; otherwise the oracle cannot show a known state effect at this length: the mediator H8-3 and the exploratory SD readouts are flagged not interpretable; H8-1 and H8-2 stay interpretable.
- **Planted cost control A5-read** (A5 plus a fixed instruction "read every source file in the repository at the start of each session", n = 10): known cost growth with size. It must be classified COSTLIER than A5 on the growth estimator at the planned n; otherwise the experiment cannot detect a known cost difference of this kind and any EQUIVALENT-type row is INVALID-DESIGN (row R6 is then withheld).
- **Systematic-drift control A5b** (independent run of A5, n = 10): the point difference between A5 and A5b on log R(30) and the growth estimator at least delta with the 95% interval excluding zero signals drift, order or window effects: INVALID-DESIGN. Limit stated: noise is measured for A5 only; per-arm SDs are reported.
- **Negative control A4-stale** (Core, n = 20, so the content claim is a direct contrast): A4 whose sentinel routing and recorded rules are wrong for a registered fraction (30% of entries), the wrong entries generated by an independent model of another vendor as plausible-but-wrong content (registered seed; a mechanical shuffle only as the fallback), identical file sizes. **The content claim requires A4 minus A4-stale positive (H8-5)**; otherwise "true content not shown" (Q2). The share of stale entries the agent repairs is logged per checkpoint; if more than half are repaired by change 10 the control is labelled "self-repaired, uninformative" and H8-5 is not evidence either way.
- **Floor and ceiling, on arm-independent evidence.** Acceptance rate of A5 at or above 0.97 in 80% of chains means durability is not tested (H8-3 and R∞ not interpretable; H8-1 and H8-2 stay cost readouts and are labelled so). If **all** arms are below acceptance 0.2 the benchmark is at the floor: INVALID-DESIGN. If only A4 is at the floor or collapses in more than 50% of its chains while the comparators are healthy, that is an **outcome** (row R7, COSTLIER or "the substrate fails on this construct"), analysed under ITT, never invalidity. Collapse above 30% in A0 flags a difficulty problem; above 50% in a **comparator or control** arm is INVALID-DESIGN for that arm's contrasts.
- **Manipulation checks M1 and M2 and parity**: inherited from SDX-1-ARMS section 4 for A5, A5b, A4; the leak check applies to A5g's hook (a hook that checks anything but "tests and strict type check pass" is a leak) and to A4-stale and A5-read. **M2 is re-run at length** on A5 (the practitioner attests the prompt applies at any size; if they supply a size-specific variant A5-L, it replaces A5 as the comparator inside the intersection-union and the strength check is run on it; otherwise every claim row carries the qualifier "rival prompt not adapted to chain length").
- **Gate-execution check:** gate executions are logged per session per arm; if fewer than `[FROM SDX-8-P]`% of A4 and A5g sessions execute their gate, "enforced" is withdrawn for that arm (Q4). **Circumvention** (A4 and A5g): `--no-verify`, edited tests or fixtures, forged lock hash; counts per arm; intention to treat; above `[FROM SDX-8-P]`% "enforced" is withdrawn.
- **Invalid-design triggers** (each tested by "which true world does this label invalid", Lessons E.2; none fires on the treatment arm's own failure): drift control; floor or ceiling of the pooled kind above; comparator collapse; failed planted cost control (for equivalence rows); canary recall above zero; model snapshot, CLI or Node changed mid-window, or the registered price table or its application script edited after freeze; harness persistence found; ballast hash mismatch; tamper-detection failure; probe-versioning error found in the manifest; practitioner independence or authorship rule broken; a cap hit that the harness classifies as infrastructure-caused (logged before unblinding). **Cap hits as such are an outcome**, reported per arm, never a trigger.

## 4. Fixture, chain and growth

### 4.1 Fixture: Pastura, shared with SDX-1

### 4.2 The chain: SDX-1's ten changes, then twenty more, with ballast

**Changes 1 to 10 are SDX-1's, with identical texts, order, scripted replies (D1 at 3, D2 at 7), supersession (6), reversal (9) and late independent feature (10).** SDX-8 therefore extends the twelve-change-request family SDX-1 draws from (SDX-1 uses nine of the original twelve and adds a late tenth) to a longer growing chain. Changes 11 to 30 are new. **Ballast** is a frozen corpus of arm-neutral inherited code (about 2 kLOC per tranche, written by an independent author, with its own tests and sealed probes, no GS vocabulary, some deliberate realism: a naming convention different from the scaffold's, a rule implemented twice, a few unremarkable warts) that the harness merges in four tranches (T1 to T4) before the sessions of changes 11, 16, 21 and 26 as a neutral commit **that bypasses every arm's hooks**; the change text announces it in identical words in every arm ("the repository has just been merged with module X from another team, directory Y"). Arm gates see ballast only when the agent touches it. Ballast probes are assigned to the merge change of their tranche (11, 16, 21, 26) and scored under the lag-3 rule, so breaking inherited code costs acceptance. Ballast makes size at a given index approximately **arm-independent** (about 8 kLOC of the final size come from ballast) while the arms' own code adds about 5 to 8 kLOC, so the final size is about 17 to 19 kLOC `[FROM SDX-8-P]`; the registered floor is 15 kLOC in every arm at change 30. Size at each snapshot is logged per chain. Ballast files may be edited by agents; the tamper check covers the scaffold only, ballast is hash-checked on insertion. The ballast corpus is designed once and shared with SDX-4 (its S, M and L tiers are prefixes; SDX-8 uses S growing to M).

### 4.3 Scored state and the length of a chain

The working tree after the last attempt of each change (SDX-1 rule); hooks may block commits but the scored state is the working tree, so a hook is not free rollback. Test deletion, weakening and fixture editing are logged in every arm. Horizon CR0 plus 30 changes, registered; a longer horizon would be a new registration.

### 4.4 Follow-on tasks (the debt observable the treatment does not target)

After change 30, from the final snapshot of every chain, three frozen follow-on tasks (independent author, matched in size, touching areas modified in each arm's own chain through a registered mechanical rule, for example the three files with the most edits) are run by a **neutral executor**: the A0 prompt, no arm artifact beyond what sits in the repository. Cost and hidden-probe pass per task are exploratory mediators, never a verdict. The repository carries each arm's persisted files, the realistic inheritance case and a stated limit (the executor may read them).

## 5. Model

One vendor, one mid-tier model through its headless CLI; dated snapshot id, CLI and Node versions recorded; all chains inside a short window (long chains make drift a larger risk than in SDX-1; the window is registered `[FROM SDX-8-P]`; a change of snapshot inside it is INVALID-DESIGN for the affected cells); arm order randomized in blocks of one chain per arm with A5b interleaved. A second vendor and a frontier cell are replications and a capacity moderator, SDX-2, not pooled. Judges (M1, parity, ballast leak, text-suitability rating, escape audit) are from another vendor than the generator; none is installed on this machine, so JC must supply API access before freezing.

## 6. Oracle (sealed, outside every repository, HTTP level)

Inherited from SDX-1 section 6 (status-class tolerance with value and list-membership checks wherever the per-change contract defines them, paired probes scored as pairs, 2xx/4xx balance overall and inside the SD subset, fields checked by the names the contract fixes, mechanical SD rule frozen before any substrate artifact is designed). Extensions: probes for changes 11 to 30 and for ballast behaviours, authored by a person who is not a builder; a **reference implementation extended through change 30 plus ballast**; at least 40 hand mutants (at least 90% killed, including mutants that live only in ballast-touching code and mutants that break an earlier change when a later one is added); "always 200" and "always 4xx" stubs each at most 60%; reference at least 98%; the SD rule re-run for changes 14 to 28 before the substrate is touched. Held-out open-field suite at chain end (restart persistence, migration of a data file written by the previous change, concurrency, calendar and daylight-saving boundaries, volume), tagged by the change whose behaviour each probe tests, never visible to any arm, **a separate instance of the registered taxonomy from the suite SDX-9 uses at S3 and from SDX-5's**, one defect log across all three instances.

**Convention tolerance and gaming.** An always-4xx server fails the paired probes; a probe may not reward one resolution of a decision point (D3 and D4 probes accept the scripted resolution and are checked by the independent reviewer for neutrality); the oracle checks behaviour only, never structure, never names beyond the contract. Targeted metrics (A4's own sensors: criteria-ledger coverage, hook blocks, layer violations when a gate enforces them, test counts) are reported labelled "targeted" and never carry a verdict. **Untargeted erosion metric:** duplication and cognitive complexity from a tool installed in no arm and run by no gate (chosen at freeze after the list of A4's sensors is fixed), taken at every checkpoint (exploratory).

## 7. Judges

No primary readout is judged. Both co-primaries use deterministic sealed probes and recorded costs only. Judges (a different vendor, two vendors for any judged number, B11 validity first) are used for: M1 leak and parity classification, the ballast leak check, the change-text suitability rating, and a **secondary independent audit of every final repository** (escaped-defect and erosion findings the probes cannot see), reported beside the deterministic open-field result, with a human spot-check of a registered fraction, no verdict. Deterministic scripts replace judges wherever they can decide.

## 8. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Controls, manipulation checks and INVALID-DESIGN triggers (sections 2b and 9) before any contrast.
2. **H8-1.** Per chain compute the registered growth estimator (log g or b, per the selection rule). Contrast A4 minus A5 and A4 minus A5g (comparators fixed now). Classify each (section 2.2); H8-1 holds in the weaker of the two classes (intersection-union). Collapsed chains are included (ITT); the result is also reported without them and rows R2 to R4 require it to hold both ways.
3. **H8-2, the crossover, by a fixed-sequence procedure (no alpha spending, order fixed now).** Per chain compute log R(k) at k in {30, 25, 20, 15, 10, 5}. Test in that order. At each checkpoint the step is **positive** if both contrasts (A4 minus A5, A4 minus A5g; A5-L in place of A5 if supplied) are positive classes (U < 0 and L < minus delta, 97.5% BCa). Stop at the first step that is not positive. **c\*** = the earliest checkpoint of the unbroken run of positive steps that ends at 30. If checkpoint 30 is not positive, the registered statement is "no crossover within the registered horizon" with the class at 30. Fixed-sequence testing keeps the familywise level at 0.025 without correction at the price that an early crossover is credited only if the later checkpoints also pass ("crossed and stayed crossed"), which is intended.
4. **Descriptive change-point fit (c\*_fit; no verdict).** At every change k from 3 to 30 compute D(k) = mean over chains of log R_A4(k) minus mean over chains of log R_comparator(k), **separately for A5 and for A5g**; fit a continuous piecewise-linear curve with one knot tau on the grid 6..28 by least squares; c*_fit = the first k at which the fitted D(k) is at or below zero and stays so; **the reported c*_fit is the later of the two fits**, both shown, with 95% intervals by chain-level bootstrap (10,000 replicates, seed registered, chains resampled within arm). The decision rows use c* only; if c*_fit and c* differ by more than one checkpoint step the result says so and nothing is chosen after the fact. A crossover in repository size is reported by mapping c* and c*_fit through the arm-neutral ballast schedule (exploratory).
5. Secondary: H8-c, H8-3 (regression-flip rate by mixed logistic model; log cost per change on arm times log size with random intercept and slope per chain), H8-4 to H8-7, VT within budget; views V2 and V3; the cache-neutral price table; ITT and completers-only; the contrast-by-currency table (where currencies point to different rows the qualifier Q5 applies).
6. Exploratory list as in section 2.3. No further correction; nothing in the secondary list carries a verdict.

## 12. Threats to validity (declared)

Proponent-authored substrate, sensors, oracle and skeleton (mitigations as SDX-1: registration, external practitioner, mechanical SD rule frozen before the substrate exists, substrate builder blind to the change texts and the ballast, parity and leak checks, independent reviewer, independent authors for ballast and all 20 new change texts, published artifacts, an independent rerun as next step; not eliminated). Authoring asymmetry between A4 (GS side) and A5, A5g (outsider). **Ballast realism and neutrality:** inherited code written by one independent author is not the diversity of real repositories; its conventions may happen to suit one arm's sentinel; the substrate frozen to SDX-1 bytes was built for 3 kLOC and part of any A4 penalty may be an artifact of ballast insertion (bypass rule, per-tranche cost report and the exclusion sensitivity address, not remove, this). **Index is confounded with size, ballast schedule and change difficulty;** size is approximately arm-neutral and logged, g_twin addresses difficulty only partly (two pairs), and index-based comparisons are about this schedule. **Durable acceptance depends on later changes:** probe versioning handles legitimate later changes; an error in the manifest would charge arms unequally and is checked in SDX-8-P. **Cost depends on pricing and caching;** the price table is pinned, a cache-neutral table and tokens are reported. **A4's cost may be dominated by gate wait and test-suite time at size,** which dollars do not capture; wall-clock is reported. **Long window:** model drift (pin and log). **A5 at length** (attestation, M2 re-run, Q7). **The comparators are chosen by us** (A5, A5g); a better advisory arm may exist. **Chains from one fixture are not independent confirmations;** SDX-8 and SDX-1 share changes 1 to 10 and are counted once; SDX-8 and SDX-9 share the fixture family, A5 bytes, practitioner and oracle machinery and **neither is support for the other**. **Toy scale and invented domain:** at most 18 kLOC. **The ratio estimand** (window sums, denominator floor 2) is noisy; the slope estimator and the selection rule are the mitigation. **Model-only:** misses novelty and abandonment, prompting and waiting time, tacit knowledge and over-trust; GS does not address the first three and the paper says so. **Intersection-union and fixed-sequence procedures are conservative;** low power for early crossovers is a property of the design, and at the central prior the registered procedure rarely reports any crossover (section 2a). **Modal outcome near the SESOI.** **Vendor-diverse critique has not happened;** the two Claude critics of SDX-8-9-REVIEW share training. **Hook execution in headless sessions** is assumed and logged (gate-execution check), not verified for the vendor CLI by this draft.
