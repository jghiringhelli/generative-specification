## 1. Question, and why this comes before everything else

JC's order of work (2026-10-05, in substance): create projects from scratch, apply the GS formulas to produce complete and correct substrates, and only then experiment on whether the substrates make a difference. The first thing to establish is that the formulas produce, with high confidence, all the elements of the substrate. The result belongs in the Field Guide and on genspec.dev.

That is a **reliability and estimation** question, not a comparison of arms. A formula is a prompt template (`F1` set up a new project, `F2` adopt in an existing project, `F4` add a feature, the inventory of the formulas audit of 2026-10-02). The question: when a person pastes the canonical formula into a coding agent and follows it, **with what probability does each required substrate element come out present and working, how does that vary by project type, model vendor, entry path and language, and when it fails, which step of which formula is responsible?**

Why it is first:

- Every later experiment (SDX-0, SDX-1 and the P1 to P4 family of the 2026-10-04 portfolio decision) measures a difference between a project with the substrate and one without. A difference is only attributable to the substrate if the substrate was installed correctly. Today the substrate arm (A4) is built by the GS side by hand. If the formulas cannot reproduce it, a field user gets something else and the experiments describe a thing nobody can obtain.
- An internal, private census of four recent author-built projects found that none had all twelve items at the end of its first day, and that several declared gates had never been shown to block anything. This is the author's own observation, descriptive and not citable; it is the reason the checker tests that each gate blocks, and not only that it exists.
- JC's own practice is to write the spec, build an MVP, then apply GS (entry path B). Path A (greenfield from a brief) is the textbook path. Both are measured.

What this study does **not** claim: that a substrate improves quality, cost or maintenance (that is SDX/P1-P4), that the elements are the right elements, or that the content of a present element is good. "Present and working" is an operational, mostly structural and behavioural standard defined by the checker (section 6) and nothing more; it is silent on the semantic quality of a specification or an architecture document except through a flagged secondary judge (section 7). Every public sentence derived from FX-1 carries that qualifier (section 11).

## 2. Estimands, hypotheses as estimation targets, thresholds

### 2.1 Definitions

- **Run**: one complete execution of an entry path on one (fixture, model, language, formula version) from an empty folder, ending in a committed git repository. Runs are independent: fresh process, fresh configuration directory, no memory, unique path. The harness never commits: work the agent leaves uncommitted is simply absent from the scored state.
- **Elements E01 to E12** (the twelve items fixed before looking at any result in the day-one analysis; mapping to the ids of `SUBSTRATE-CHECKLIST` in the formulas branch is a freeze dependency, section 13 item 2): E01 sentinel that routes to other files; E02 spec with requirement ids and acceptance criteria with ids (the checker reports 2a and 2b separately); E03 decision records; E04 derived cascade (architecture, data model, conventions) routed from the sentinel; E05 tests plus an enforced blocking gate; E06 ratchet (a measured floor that cannot go down); E07 open-questions gate; E08 criteria coverage tracking; E09 atomic, descriptive, conventional commits; E10 spec lock; E11 co-change gate; E12 clean-clone executability instruction in the README.
- **Element result** (per run and element): the checker status `PASS` (present **and** working) versus anything else (`PARTIAL`, `ABSENT`). `UNDETERMINABLE` is a checker or environment fault, resolved by the rules of section 9 before analysis. Definitions of "working" per element: `FX-1-CHECKER-SPEC.md` section 3. E09 is scored on the commits made **after the formula's first step** (the harness records the boundary commit: the empty initial commit on path A, the head of the neutral MVP on path B), because on path B the earlier history is written by the neutral build prompt, not by the formula; whole-history E09 is reported as descriptive.
- **Stream**: entry path x language, four streams: A-EN, A-ES, B-EN, B-ES. English and neutral Spanish are separate streams because the prompt text, the brief and the generated documents are different artifacts; no stream is pooled with another in any primary quantity.
- **Cell**: fixture x model within a stream (5 x 3 = 15 cells per stream), 14 runs each (section 4).
- **The estimand is conditional on the registered design.** pi(e,s) is the probability that a run of stream s yields `PASS` for element e, averaged with equal weight over the 15 registered cells (the design is balanced, so it equals the pooled proportion). It is not a population parameter of "projects" or "models": with five fixtures and three vendors a population claim is not supported (the vendor-to-vendor variance is not estimable from three vendors). Every public sentence therefore says "for these three models on these five project types", and the per-vendor lower bounds are reported as the statement about vendors. theta(s) is the probability that a run of stream s yields `PASS` on all twelve elements.

### 2.2 Intervals and what "high confidence" means

The verdict interval is the **Wilson 95% interval** on the pooled count of the stream (n = 210 runs). It is the right interval for a fixed, balanced design (stratified sampling of the registered cells makes it conservative). Two further quantities are reported beside it and can only **qualify** a result, never create one:

1. **Heterogeneity flag**: a cell bootstrap (resample the 15 cells, 10,000 replicates, percentile method, seed registered) describes how much the rate varies between cells. If the Wilson lower bound is at least 0.90 but the bootstrap lower bound is below 0.90, the element is labelled `MEETS-UNEVEN` and its public wording names the weakest cell. The bootstrap is descriptive: a percentile bootstrap over 15 crossed, non-exchangeable cells is not a valid interval for a population.
2. **Audit sensitivity** (a registered sensitivity analysis, not a conjunct of the verdict): the human audit of section 6 (V-C4) estimates the checker's false-`PASS` rate **and** false-`PARTIAL` rate per element, pooled across streams, each with a Clopper-Pearson interval. The Wilson lower bound is recomputed after subtracting the false-`PASS` upper limit times the `PASS` count (conservative lenient-checker case), and the upper bound after adding back the false-`PARTIAL` upper limit times the non-`PASS` count (harsh-checker case). A `RELIABLE` result whose audit-adjusted lower bound is also at least 0.90 is labelled "audited" in public wording; one that is not is labelled "audit-sensitive". Why not a conjunct: with zero errors found among 100 audited `PASS` judgments the false-`PASS` upper limit is 0.036, and an adjusted bound of 0.90 then needs at least 205 of 210 passes (simulation, `results.md`), which would make `RELIABLE` unreachable for a perfect checker unless its true rate exceeds 0.98.

**High confidence** (operational definition, the only meaning the word has in FX-1): the Wilson lower bound for the stream is at least **0.90**. That is the rung `RELIABLE`.

Why 0.90 and why a lower bound, written before data:

- A formula is something a person follows once; the cost of a miss is a repeat pass. At 0.90 per element, with the formula's own verification step catching the miss (the formulas audit requires a "how to check it worked" step in every formula), two passes reach 0.99 per element. A bar below 0.80 would make a typical user expect a missing element in more than one project of five; a bar above 0.95 cannot be certified at an affordable n (n = 210 certifies one element with probability 0.99 only if its true rate is 0.97 or higher).
- A lower bound and not the point estimate: with 210 runs a stream with 195 passes has a point estimate of 0.93 and a Wilson lower bound of 0.89; the claim a reader can rely on is the bound.
- 0.90 is a convention, not a derivation from user harm. JC may change it before freeze; the simulation shows what each choice costs. After freeze it cannot move.

Rungs (a classification of the same interval, not extra hypotheses): `RELIABLE` lower bound >= 0.90; `USUALLY` >= 0.80; `OFTEN` >= 0.50; `NOT-YET` below 0.50. Public wording is tied to the rung (section 11).

**Multiplicity.** The conjunctive claim CONJ-s (below) is an intersection-union: it is accepted only if all twelve individual bounds pass, so its error probability is bounded by that of one test and no correction is needed. The up to 48 individual element-stream statements published on formula pages are **not** family-controlled: each carries its own 95 percent bound, the probability that at least one of the 48 is falsely `RELIABLE` is not controlled, and the formula pages say so.

### 2.3 Registered estimation targets

| Id | Target | Registered threshold | Fails (falsifier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-e-s (12 elements x 4 streams) | pi(e,s), the per-element rate | `MEETS` if the Wilson lower bound >= 0.90; `FAILS-TARGET` if the upper Wilson bound < 0.90; otherwise `UNRESOLVED` at 0.90 and the rung is reported; `MEETS-UNEVEN` and "audited" labels as in 2.2 | `FAILS-TARGET` for that element and stream |
| CONJ-s (4 streams) | every one of the twelve elements `MEETS` (intersection-union) | all twelve `MEETS` | any element not `MEETS` |
| COMPLETE-s (4 streams) | theta(s), all twelve present and working in the same run | rung as above; `USUALLY` (lower bound >= 0.80) is the registered acceptable level, `RELIABLE` the stretch level | lower bound < 0.80 |
| W | interval width goal | Wilson 95% full width at most 0.10 whenever the stream point estimate is at least 0.90 (n = 210 gives 0.08) | n or interval wider |

CONJ and COMPLETE are separate on purpose (a correction after review: revision 1 required both in one claim, which at independent per-element rates of 0.97 is almost never met). The headline "a complete substrate" needs COMPLETE-s, and COMPLETE-s at 0.80 needs each element to be about 0.99 if failures are independent (twelve elements at 0.99 give 0.89 jointly; at 0.97, 0.69). Section 4.3 gives the joint simulation.

Why COMPLETE-s threshold 0.80 and not 0.90: it means "four projects in five come out complete"; 0.90 jointly implies about 0.99 per element, which no affordable n can certify unless the true rate is about 0.995. If JC wants 0.90, the claim becomes reachable only at true per-element rates above 0.995.

Descriptive, no threshold: mean number of elements `PASS` per run and its distribution; the partial-versus-absent split per element; cost and turns per run; questions the agent asked; differences between English and Spanish (Newcombe intervals), between vendors and between fixtures (section 8).

### 2.4 Prior expectations, stated so a result cannot later be called "as expected"

From the mixed evidence of the lab (one observation per template, Claude only, mid-tier) and the day-one census: easy elements (E01 sentinel, E02 spec, E03 decisions, E09 commits, E12 README) are expected near 0.90 to 0.99; the elements that need executed, verified behaviour (E05 enforced gate, E06 ratchet, E07 open-questions gate, E10 lock, E11 co-change) are expected much lower, 0.3 to 0.8, because the formulas for them are least tested and because a gate that is declared is not the same as a gate that blocks. The modal outcome is therefore **not** CONJ met: it is several elements `RELIABLE`, several `FAILS-TARGET`, a few `UNRESOLVED`, and COMPLETE `NOT-YET`. By the authors' own prior most of the 840 confirmatory runs would confirm a predicted failure on a formula version that will then be rewritten. That is why the design has a **diagnostic stage first** (FX-0, section 3.4): it finds the clear failures cheaply, allows a pre-declared revision of the formula text, and only the revised, frozen text meets the 840-run confirmatory stage.

## 3. Entry paths, formulas, arms

**Path A, greenfield from a brief.** Empty folder; the harness gives the fixture brief and runs formula F1 (set up the substrate in a new project), then formula F4 three times, one per feature of the brief's "first slice". End state: a committed repository with a first slice built under the substrate.

**Path B, brief to MVP to formulas (JC's practice).** Step 1: a neutral build prompt (fixed text, section 3.2) builds a working MVP of the first slice from the brief with no GS content; step 2: formula F2 (adopt GS in an existing project) is run on that repository. End state as in A.

The formulas are the **frozen tagged versions** of the formula folder (section 13 item 1): `formulas-v1` or whatever tag the formulas branch ships, with the English text and the registered neutral-Spanish text each hashed. FX-1 runs the formulas exactly as written: the harness fills only the `[brackets]` (project name, one paragraph from the brief, stack from the brief) by a fixed script, sends the formula text as the user's message, and sends the next step of a multi-step formula when the agent's turn **ends without a tool call** (the registered definition of "finished"). Any formula step addressed to the human (run a command, ratify a document) is performed by the harness as a scripted action listed in the coverage map; the number of such steps is a descriptive variable. When the agent asks the user a question, the harness replies with one fixed sentence ("Use your best judgment from the brief, record the assumption in the project documents, and do not ask again."); the number of questions is logged. This is a registered departure from a real user, who would answer in detail, and is listed as a threat.

Three arms exist; only the first carries verdicts:

| Arm | What the model is told | Role | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| FORM | the canonical formula, verbatim | confirmatory (all of section 2.3) | 840 runs (4 streams x 210) |
| F0 | one generic sentence: "Set this project up so a coding assistant can maintain it for years: documentation, tests, safety checks, history." plus the brief (path A) or the existing repository (path B) | attribution control: an element is `NOT-ATTRIBUTABLE` to the formula unless FORM exceeds F0 by at least 0.20 with a Newcombe interval excluding zero, per path | 120 runs, English only (5 fixtures x 3 vendors x 2 paths x 4); Spanish attribution is untested and stated as such |
| CHK | the machine-checkable definitions of the twelve elements (the formulas' `SUBSTRATE-CHECKLIST`, as plain requirements, no formula steps) | capability ceiling for the English streams only (not applied to Spanish): tells "requirements alone are not enough" from "even told exactly, the element is not reached" | 90 runs, English only, 3 per cell and path |

F0 and CHK are descriptive; they never carry a verdict. CHK cannot exempt a formula (section 9.3, item 6).

### 3.1 Prompt language

English and neutral Spanish are registered as separate artifacts: separate formula texts (the Spanish text is the registered translation, hash in the freeze list; the register is neutral tuteo, not voseo, a decision of JC, section 13 item 8), separate briefs (the Spanish brief is a translation of the English brief made once, reviewed by a native reader before freeze), separate streams, separate intervals. The Spanish checker configuration is the same file; its Spanish heading patterns and **path candidates** are validated by Spanish controls (checker spec section 5); the Spanish known-good control is built by a person who did not translate the English one (V-C1). Unless the Spanish formula forces English file paths, a Spanish project that uses Spanish paths must be recognised by the checker; both options are decided before freeze (section 13 item 8).

### 3.2 The neutral build prompt (path B step 1)

"Build the following product as a working first version in the current folder. Use git and commit your work as you go. Choose sensible tools within the stated stack. Brief: [brief]". Spanish: the registered translation. It names no GS element and asks for nothing the elements test.

### 3.3 Where and how runs are executed

The agent is run **inside the same pinned Linux container image as the checker** (Node 24, Python 3.11 with pytest, git, bash, network limited to the package registries and the sources a hook framework needs, which the registration lists). Reason: a project built on Windows would carry non-executable hooks and CRLF shebangs that fail on the Linux checker for reasons the agent could never observe; that would be a systematic bias against the formulas.

### 3.4 FX-0, the diagnostic and validity stage (84 runs, excluded from the confirmatory analysis)

FX-0 has two parts and runs before FX-1, under the protocol's stage 4 (pilot):

- **Diagnostic part, 60 runs**: one run per cell of all four streams (5 fixtures x 3 vendors x 4 streams). Its purpose: find formula steps that clearly fail (an element with an upper Wilson bound below 0.90 in a stream, at n = 15 that needs about 11 or fewer passes of 15), measure cost per run, turns and time, and exercise the harness. A **pre-declared revision budget**: at most three revisions of each formula text, each triggered only by such a failure, each made by the formula's author, who sees the failing element's transcripts and never a rate comparison between vendors; each revision is listed with its trigger in the logbook before FX-1 is frozen. The frozen text is the one FX-1 runs.
- **Held-out part, 24 runs** (6 per stream, a different seed and different fixture-vendor pairings from the diagnostic part where possible): not used for any repair. It is the **validation sample of the checker** (V-C4): the checker may be repaired from the diagnostic part and from the controls; after the last checker change the held-out part must meet the agreement rule without further repair.

Caps for the main study are fixed by rule from the diagnostic part before FX-1 freezes: per-step turn cap and per-run wall-clock cap = 3 times the 90th percentile of the diagnostic runs per path, computed by script without reference to any element outcome.

### 4.1 Fixtures (five types, invented, written independently of GS)

### 4.3 Sample size, from simulation

`experiments\fx1\simulation\simulate.js` (Node only, seeded; exact binomial enumeration where possible, Monte Carlo elsewhere; all success rates are scenario assumptions, not measurements; full tables in `results.md`).

**Width of the Wilson 95% interval (full width) by n** (observed rate 0.95 and 0.80): n = 10: 0.28 and 0.45; 30: 0.16 and 0.28; 50: 0.12 and 0.22; 100: 0.09 and 0.16; 150: 0.07 and 0.13; 200: 0.06 and 0.11; 210: 0.06 and 0.11; 300: 0.05 and 0.09. A cell of 14 runs is therefore descriptive only (width about 0.3); decisions are taken per stream.

**Planned n per stream = 210** (5 fixtures x 3 vendors x 14 runs), 840 runs in FORM. Reason: the smallest tested n at which (i) the width goal W holds (0.08 at an observed rate of 0.90 or higher), (ii) one element at a true rate of 0.97 is certified `RELIABLE` with probability 0.99 (n = 150 gives 0.92, n = 180 gives 0.98), and (iii) CONJ is reachable when every element truly sits at 0.97 or higher.

| True rate of an element (n = 210) | P(`RELIABLE`: bound >= 0.90) | P(`FAILS-TARGET`: upper < 0.90) | P(interval contains 0.90, indecisive) | P(clears the 0.80 rung) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **pessimistic 0.80** | 0.000 | 0.987 | 0.013 | 0.021 |
| 0.85 | 0.000 | 0.643 | 0.357 | 0.432 |
| 0.90 | 0.019 | 0.030 | 0.951 | 0.982 |
| **central 0.93** | 0.285 | 0.000 | 0.715 | 1.000 |
| 0.95 | 0.746 | 0.000 | 0.254 | 1.000 |
| **optimistic 0.97** | 0.989 | 0.000 | 0.011 | 1.000 |
| 0.98 and above | 1.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 |

**Honest reading, because JC's rule is that no experiment should have an inconclusive probable result.** FX-1 returns a decisive answer about the 0.90 target unless a true rate lies in about 0.86 to 0.95, a band that no affordable n resolves (n = 600 per stream, about three times the cost, still leaves 0.28 indecisive at a true 0.93). In that band the study still returns a decisive statement one rung down: at a true 0.93 the lower bound clears 0.80 with probability 1.00 (so "at least 80%, 95% confidence" is certified even when "at least 90%" is not). The design fails to be conclusive about 90% only when an element truly sits near 90%, and it says so in the registered row (c) of section 10. By the authors' own prior (section 2.4) the elements that need executed behaviour are expected well below 0.85, where the study is decisive in the other direction.

**The headline is the hard one, and revision 1 mis-stated it.** Joint simulation (12 elements per run, 600 repetitions, n = 210; `results.md`): if every element truly sits at 0.97 and failures are independent, all twelve elements `MEET` with probability 0.88 (CONJ) but COMPLETE (all-twelve rate bound >= 0.80) is never met (0.97 to the twelfth is 0.69); at 0.98 CONJ is met with 0.997 and COMPLETE with 0.003; at 0.99 both are met with 0.92 (independent) and 0.98 (a run-quality factor); at 0.995 with 1.00. If failures are all-or-nothing within a run (a run is either clean or not) COMPLETE follows CONJ: 0.74 at 0.95 and 0.985 at 0.97. So **"every element at least 90%" is certifiable when elements are truly about 0.98; "complete substrate in 80% of runs" is certifiable only when elements are truly about 0.99 or the failures are strongly clustered by run**. A stream that is merely good (0.95 per element) is reported element by element and not as complete. That is the correct behaviour of a registered bar, stated here so a miss is not read as a failed experiment.

Project-level rate theta(s) against 0.80 at n = 210: true 0.50 or 0.65: `FAILS` with probability 1.00 and 0.998; true 0.75: `FAILS` 0.43, indecisive 0.57; true 0.85: `MEETS` 0.43; true 0.90: `MEETS` 0.98; true 0.95: 1.00.

Audit: with zero errors among 100 audited `PASS` judgments the false-`PASS` upper limit is 0.036; 150 gives 0.024; 200 gives 0.018 (`results.md`). The main-study audit is **100 runs** (section 6, V-C4), about 1,200 element judgments.

Totals: FORM 840 runs; F0 120 and CHK 90 (descriptive); FX-0 84 (excluded); **about 1,130 runs**. A stream cannot be dropped after freeze, except under the stop rule of section 14 which is registered now.

## 5. Procedure per run (frozen in the harness; hashes in the freeze list)

1. Inside the pinned container (section 3.3): create a unique empty folder; `git init`; a fresh configuration directory with auto-memory off, no user instruction files, no MCP, no web, no resume between runs (resume inside a run is the multi-step formula conversation). Pin the Node, Python and CLI versions; planted-memory check as in SDX-0 V13.
2. Fill the formula brackets with the fixed script; send the steps as in section 3. Per-step and per-run caps by the rule of section 3.4; a cap hit is scored as is.
3. After the last step: record the final commit and the boundary commit; copy the repository to the archive; run the **checker** (section 6) on the committed state, in a container started from the same image; store the full report.
4. Order of runs randomised in blocks over cells; formulas, briefs and model ids hash-verified before every batch by the registered dry check.
5. Store raw transcripts, repositories and checker reports untouched, whatever the outcome (guard i).

## 6. The instrument and its validity conditions (a precondition of the study)

The checker (`FX-1-CHECKER-SPEC.md`) is deterministic: no model judges presence or working status. It clones the committed state, installs from the README steps, runs the project's own tests, plants violations **each paired with a clean control**, tries to commit and push them, and reads the git history. The study cannot be frozen unless the checker has passed:

- **V-C1 positive controls.** At least five known-good substrates covering the five fixture types, both stacks and a pre-push-only design, **at least two built by someone who did not write the checker and who worked from the `SUBSTRATE-CHECKLIST` only, not from the checker specification**, all twelve `PASS`; plus Spanish known-goods, one of them with Spanish paths. (The prototype ships four by the checker's author, so this condition is open.)
- **V-C2 negative controls.** For every element, a variant with the element removed and a variant with it present but broken; the checker must flag exactly the declared set (the element and its declared dependents) and nothing else. The prototype ships 42 variants (see the checker spec section 5); the registered version needs at least 2 broken variants per element, authored in part by the independent reviewer, and fixture-shaped variants (a server, a CLI, a pipeline, a game engine, an MCP server). The dependency expectations are derived from element definitions, not from the architecture of one control (checker spec 5.2).
- **V-C3 gaming controls.** **Must be rejected** (`PARTIAL` or `ABSENT`): hooks that print and exit 0; a syntax-only hook; a lint script that fails at baseline and nothing else; a hook that freezes the whole spec folder; a ratchet file nothing reads; criteria cited only in a distant comment; skipped or todo tests; a sentinel linking to empty files. **Declared leaks, expected `PASS` and pinned by controls so their existence is public**: filler documents of the minimum length that cite the spec; a ratchet that rejects any edit of its file. A fake that is neither in the first list nor declared a leak at freeze fails V-C3.
- **V-C4 audit.** The **held-out part of FX-0** (24 runs, 288 element judgments) is judged by an independent human who sees the **purpose of each element and the checker's probe log, not the checker's patterns**, and is blind to the checker's status; the same written purpose-level definitions are published. Required: overall agreement at least 0.95; zero unresolved false-`PASS`; a false-`PARTIAL` rate whose upper limit is registered per element (an element with an upper limit above 0.10 has its `FAILS-TARGET` labelled uninterpretable). After the last repair no further repair is allowed before the main study. In the main study 100 runs, stratified by stream and cell, with `PASS`-heavy runs over-sampled, are audited the same way (about 40 hours of human time); both error rates feed the sensitivity analysis of section 2.2.
- **V-C5 determinism and non-interference.** Two checker runs on one repository give identical statuses; the checker never changes the repository under test (both are tests in the prototype and pass); a server-shaped control confirms no orphan processes survive a run.
- **V-C6 environment.** The authoritative checker runs on Linux in the pinned container (the executable-bit check of installed hooks and the shell behaviour differ on Windows; the prototype's tests run on Windows 11 and are not the authority for those two behaviours). A Windows-authored control (hooks committed with mode 100644) has a declared expected result.

If any V-C condition fails and cannot be repaired the study is `INVALID-DESIGN` and the freeze does not happen (section 10 row v).

## 7. Secondary, flagged, never in a verdict

Two different-vendor model judges, twice each (four judgments per item, agreement reported, flagged as inferential), rate on a 10 percent sample two things the checker cannot: whether E02 criteria are testable statements and whether the E04 documents are in fact derived from the spec (the checker only tests a proxy: the document cites the spec path or one of its ids). The judges see only the artifacts, never the stream or vendor of the generator, and are not the generating vendor of the run being judged. Their results are reported beside the structural results as an upper bound on quality; they cannot move an element from `PASS` to `FAIL`.

## 8. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Validity checks (section 10 row v) and UNDETERMINABLE resolution (section 9) before any rate.
2. Per stream: for each element, the count of `PASS`, the Wilson interval, the rung, `MEETS`/`FAILS-TARGET`/`UNRESOLVED`, the heterogeneity flag, the audit sensitivity labels; the partial-versus-absent split; the project-level rate theta(s) and the distribution of the number of elements `PASS`; CONJ-s and COMPLETE-s.
3. Diagnosis tables (descriptive, section 9): for each non-`MEETS` element, rates by vendor and by fixture with Wilson intervals; formula step from the coverage map; F0 and CHK rates with intervals and the F0 contrast.
4. English versus Spanish: difference in proportions per element with a Newcombe interval; labelled exploratory when an interval excludes zero (no verdict).
5. Nothing else is a verdict. Everything not listed is exploratory and labelled.
6. Scripts and seeds are in the freeze list; the analysis is run by script on the archived reports. Sensitivity analyses registered: (i) strict enforcement (an enforcement element counts only if blocked at commit or push, scripts not credited); (ii) CI-only gates credited when the workflow is statically valid; (iii) E09 on the whole history.

## 9. Pre-registered treatment of failures

Three different things can be wrong; the registration fixes in advance how each is told apart, so that no failure is relabelled after the fact.

**9.1 Checker failures (the instrument is wrong).**

- `UNDETERMINABLE` (a network failure on install, a tool missing, a timeout): the checker re-runs once in a fresh sandbox. If still undetermined, a human adjudicates that element for that run, blind to vendor. If more than 3 percent of the run-element results of any element end up adjudicated this way, that element's estimate is declared `INVALID-DESIGN` for FX-1 (the checker is not fit for it) and is not interpreted.
- Audit disagreement (V-C4 and the main-study sample): a disagreement is classified false-`PASS` or false-`PARTIAL`. A false-`PASS` upper limit of 0.05 or more invalidates that element's `MEETS`; a false-`PARTIAL` upper limit above 0.10 makes its `FAILS-TARGET` uninterpretable.
- A systematic checker defect found after data: the checker version is bumped and **all** runs are re-scored with the fixed checker; both sets of results are published and the deviations log records it. Asymmetry is removed: for a defect classified false-`PASS` (lenient) the verdict uses the less favourable of the two sets; for a defect classified false-`PARTIAL` (harsh) the verdict uses the corrected checker, provided the fix is a convention addition validated by a new control and confirmed by the human auditor on the audited sample.

**9.2 Harness and infrastructure failures (not the formula, not the model).**

A run that did not reach the formula's last step because of an infrastructure fault (vendor outage, rate limit, process killed by the harness, disk) is re-run once in the same slot; the original is archived and counted in the failure log. Infrastructure loss above 5 percent in any stream before any outcome data are seen is a stop-and-amend trigger (the interim validity look); above 5 percent found afterwards is `INVALID-DESIGN` for that stream (row v): one event, one consequence depending on whether data exist. A differential loss above 10 percentage points between vendors is the same. A run that ended because of the agent (it stopped, asked to continue, hit the turn or time cap) is **not** an infrastructure failure: it is scored as is and counts against the formula (intention to treat), because a user following the formula would experience it.

**9.3 Formula failures versus model failures (the diagnosis; it never changes a rate).**

Every non-`PASS` run-element result is a failure for the rate. The cause label is diagnostic and fixed by this cascade, in order, **per element and stream**:

1. **CHECKER-CONVENTION**: the element was not probed or not found because the checker could not locate the target (no source directory it recognises, paths in another language, an id grammar it does not parse). It routes to row (h), not to a formula or a project type.
2. **FORMULA-OMISSION**: the frozen formula contains no step that instructs this element (the static coverage map, built and hashed before any run). A formula that omits an element has an expected rate near zero and is reported as incomplete.
3. **MODEL-SPECIFIC**: the formula instructs the element, one vendor's rate is at least 0.20 below the pooled rate of the other two and a Fisher exact test across vendors gives p below 0.01.
4. **PROJECT-TYPE**: one fixture's rate is at least 0.25 below the others and Fisher p below 0.01, **and** the checker's probe log shows the target was found (otherwise label 1 applies).
5. **FORMULA-STEP**: the formula instructs the element, the element is `FAILS-TARGET` (or the stream rate is below 0.80), and labels 3 and 4 are negative. Attributed to that formula step; the report names the step and splits the failures into `ABSENT` (the instruction was not followed: wording, length, ordering) and `PARTIAL` (the artifact exists but does not work: the step lacks a self-test). A uniform shortfall across vendors and fixtures is the strongest evidence of a formula problem.
6. **RUN-VARIANCE**: only for an element that is `UNRESOLVED` (not `FAILS-TARGET`); scattered failures that no label above explains.

The CHK arm **cannot exempt a formula**. If CHK also stays below 0.80 for an element (an upper Wilson bound below 0.80 per path, English only) the conclusion is "requirements alone do not produce this element"; the formula's claim for the element stays `FAILS-TARGET` and is published as such; an audit of the element's definition (row h) is mandatory before the formula step is rewritten, because a harsh definition would produce the same pattern. "Infeasible" is not a label FX-1 can assign: it would need a stronger ceiling arm (checklist plus a reference gate implementation, or a frontier model) that this study does not contain.

## 11. Where results go and how they are worded

Every closed FX entry follows the LOGBOOK template; public pages state the outcome label and tier verbatim. **Every rung sentence carries the qualifier**: "met the FX-1 checker's definition of present and working (structural and behavioural; content quality not assessed)". The ladder: `RELIABLE` -> "with an estimated probability of at least 90 percent (lower 95 percent bound)"; `USUALLY` -> "at least 80 percent"; `OFTEN` -> "at least half"; `NOT-YET` -> "not yet reliably". The wording says "an estimated probability", never "in at least 90 percent of runs" (the bound is about the underlying probability, not a sample frequency). The words "proven", "guaranteed" and "complete substrate" are not used unless CONJ-s and COMPLETE-s are met for that stream, and then with the scope sentence of row (a). Failure modes are published with the rate, never in a separate appendix.

## 12. Threats to validity (declared)

Everything is GS-side: formulas, checker, fixtures, elements and thresholds were written by the same side; the independent reader of the fixtures, the independent author of some controls and the independent auditor are mitigations, not yet in place. The formula authors may know the checker's probes (the specification is public in the same repository), so a passing formula may be tuned to the checker; the result then says "meets this operational definition". The twelve elements are the GS side's definition of a substrate; FX-1 does not test that they matter. "Present and working" is largely structural and behavioural: a document can pass with weak content (the secondary judges bound this; they are inferential), and two leaks are declared and pinned by controls (checker spec section 7). Enforcement elements other than E05 credit a failing script that passes at baseline, not only a hook (sensitivity analysis registered): this is the one place the checker is more lenient than "a hook blocks". The python fixtures reach those elements only by hook, the node fixtures also by script, an asymmetry the PROJECT-TYPE label could mistake for a property of the project type. Elements 10 and 11 (spec lock, co-change gate) are design-status in the canon; if the formula does not instruct them the result is FORMULA-OMISSION, which may be a correct outcome. The briefs are close to specifications (section 4.1). The scripted answers replace a real person's answers. Mid-tier models only; one date; vendor drift; five fixtures designed by one author with similar behaviour counts; two stacks; the checker runs headless and installs packages (a network-dependent step); `UNDETERMINABLE` handling is a place where judgment enters (bounded by the 3 percent rule). The F0 and CHK arms are small and descriptive; Spanish has no F0 or CHK. The estimand is conditional on the registered cells; the 48 element-stream statements are not family-controlled. The Spanish brief and formula are translations by the GS side. All success rates in section 4.3 are assumptions. The human audit adopts the checker's purpose-level definitions and can still import its conventions. **Vendor-diverse review of this design is owed and absent.**

## 14. Cost and time (estimates; the unit costs are guesses until FX-0 measures them)

A formula run is several agent sessions long: path A about 120 turns (F1 then three F4), path B about 110 turns (build then F2). The lab measured $0.18 to $0.43 per 28-turn session on a mid-tier model, about $0.007 to $0.015 per turn; vendor differences are assumed to span a factor of 0.5 to 2.5. That gives $0.7 to $4 per run, mean about $2. FORM 840 runs: about $1,700 (range $600 to $3,400); F0 and CHK 210 runs at about $1.2: $250 (range $90 to $500); FX-0 84 runs: about $170; secondary judges (four judgments on about 84 runs): about $60 to $170. **Total central about $2,300, range about $950 to $4,300; hard cap $3,500** (JC's per-experiment default of 2026-10-04).

**Registered stop rule.** The cap and the per-run cost are taken from FX-0 before freeze (the 90th percentile per-run cost times the planned runs must not exceed the cap). If it does, the registered reduced design is: streams A-EN and B-EN at the full 210 each (never fewer runs per cell), F0 and CHK unchanged in English, and the Spanish streams deferred to a linked FX-1c; the decision probabilities are unchanged for the retained streams. An interim validity look after the first 15 percent of runs, by a script that prints only flags (cost per run above 1.7 times the FX-0 estimate, infrastructure loss, UNDETERMINABLE rate, canary), triggers this reduction **before** any element rate is looked at; after outcome data exist no amendment is allowed (protocol section 5).

Wall clock: about 1,130 runs at 4 in parallel; agent time per run is taken from FX-0 (planned 10 to 25 minutes) plus 1 to 2 minutes of checker time: about 70 to 150 hours, so vendor drift within one date is a real exposure and the model ids are pinned. Human time: independent reader of briefs about 6 hours; control authoring about 8 hours; auditor about 50 hours (288 judgments at FX-0 held-out, about 1,200 on the 100-run sample, at 2 to 3 minutes each with the probe log); JC's decisions about 3 hours; preparation (per-vendor runners, container, harness, hash scripts, formula revisions during FX-0) about 80 to 120 agent-assisted hours. The preparation, not the model spend, is the larger cost.
