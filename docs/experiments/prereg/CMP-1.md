## 1. Question and what it is for

**On one invented, medium-small project, when the same two frontier models build the project from the same plain-language brief and then absorb ten further change requests (six with planted traps), how do three ways of working differ in (a) behaviour that sealed tests accept, (b) planted traps handled correctly, (c) tokens and dollars including setup and upkeep, and (d) how much a human must read to approve the change? The three ways of working are a bare model, GitHub spec-kit used as its own documentation recommends, and Generative Specification (GS) used as its own formulas prescribe.**

It is an **estimation pilot**. It exists to (1) decide, by a rule registered in advance (section 12, item 6), whether a larger fair comparison is worth its cost, (2) measure the variances that a larger design needs, (3) give a first, honestly bounded, public statement about what was measured, and (4) test that the comparison machinery is itself fair (controls, section 9). It tests nothing about team workflows, human productivity, long horizons, governance value, other tools, other stacks or other task families.

Why this comparison and why now: the landscape report found the category crowded and free; spec-kit (MIT, about 140,000 stars on 2026-10-08) is the most visible tool; almost all of the category is guidance the model reads, and the open question is whether anything measurable differs. The GS side has a stake in the answer and wrote this protocol, which is why sections 15 and 16 exist.

Priors, stated as priors and not results: (1) Earlier GS work on single-shot builds shows ties on behaviour (AX, AX2 saturated; MX), and a generation-cost multiplier of about 2.9 for the disciplined arm with no single-shot behavioural return. (2) Frontier models navigate unfamiliar code natively (SX pilot), so a bare arm may be strong on a small project. (3) The GS thesis is that enforced, executed checks (hooks, ratchet, lock, co-change gate) reduce regressions across fresh sessions; that is a hypothesis of the GS side and the only place where a difference is expected. (4) Spec-kit's own checks (analyze, converge, constitution gates) are model-run passes; community add-ons that enforce outside the model exist and are **not** in this pilot (section 15, threat 6). (5) Boeckeler's review-burden critique of spec-driven tools predicts a larger review surface for both tool arms.

## 2. Hypotheses, as estimation plus falsifiers (registered; priors are priors)

Notation: the three arms are **B** (bare), **K** (spec-kit), **G** (GS). Contrasts are G-B, K-B and G-K, each as a difference of pooled means over the two vendors (equal weight), with per-vendor differences always shown beside the pooled one. Margin for a "look-worthy" difference: **Delta = 10 points** on the two quality outcomes (a ratio of 1.25 on cost). Delta is a pilot threshold for deciding whether to spend more, not a claim of practical importance. Quality outcomes: **P-ACC2** and **P-TRAP** (section 7). Class names (AHEAD, BEHIND, TIE and so on) are defined once, in section 13.

**Falsifier power is stated, not assumed.** For each row, the simulation (`results.md` section 3) gives the probability that the falsifier fires when the prior is true and when it is false. A falsifier that fires with probability below about 0.5 when the prior is false cannot refute that prior at k = 5; such rows are marked **weak**, and a non-firing is never support.

**Pre-stated outcomes** (written before any run; assigned mechanically by section 13; the names in brackets are for this document only, public wording is in section 14):

- **O1 [the "GS loses" outcome]:** G is BEHIND K or B on a quality outcome (the other arm is AHEAD of G), or G is COSTLIER than K or B with both quality outcomes TIE for that pair. Both conditions require evidence; "not AHEAD" alone is not evidence of loss.
- **O2 [the "spec-kit wins" outcome, mirror image]:** the same conditions with K as the tool and G or B as the comparator.
- **O3 [bare holds]:** neither tool is AHEAD or SUGGESTED against B on either quality outcome, and B is CHEAPER than both tools. The expected outcome at this size and stated as a possibility in earnest: it means that specification overhead bought no measured gain on this task.
- **O4 / O5:** G (or K) is AHEAD of at least one comparator on a quality outcome and BEHIND on none. Reported with the same words as any other contrast.
- **O6:** a pair is TIE on both quality outcomes (the sketch says k = 5 rarely reaches this).
- **O9 (cost line, not a loss):** a tool is COSTLIER than a comparator and the quality outcomes are unresolved (INCONCLUSIVE or SUGGESTED). This is the expected line under the section 11 cost prior; it says the pilot saw the cost and could not resolve the quality.
- **O7 mixed, O8 uninformative, O0 residual:** section 13.

The same evidential bar applies to both tools: O1 and O2 are mirror images with the same thresholds, and every "without detected gain" statement prints the pilot's power for a 20-point difference (section 10).

## 3. Arms: each tool as its own documentation recommends

Common to all arms: the same starting repository (the locked scaffold, section 4, identical bytes), the same models and vendors, the same tools (read, edit, shell, git, test runner; web and MCP disabled), the same sealed hidden tests, the same change-request texts (written by the independent author; each arm wraps them only as its own method prescribes), the same scripted human (section 5), and the same neutral engineering preamble (identical bytes: work in this repository, keep existing behaviour working and the public interface unchanged unless the request says otherwise, run the project's tests, commit when done). **The preamble is prepended only to the message that carries the brief or the change text, never to a skill or formula control message** (it would otherwise interfere with each method's own sequencing); its effect is checked in CMP-0. The conversation unit is: **one conversation per phase 1 build, and one fresh conversation per change request in phase 2**; the repository carries all state.

**Caps are per arm.** A per-session and a per-run cap in tokens and wall-clock, set for each arm as three times that arm's median use in CMP-0 `[FROM CMP-0]`, so that no arm is capped where its method is long by design; the converge/implement iteration cap of K is 3 `[FROM CMP-0]`. Cap-hit steps are reported per arm beside both quality outcomes, with a sensitivity that excludes them; a cap-hit share above 15% of steps in any arm of CMP-0 raises that arm's cap by 50% and repeats that CMP-0 cell. A cap hit scores the tree as it stands.

## 4. The task and the fixture

**Type.** One invented, non-memorized product of medium-small size, built by the arms from a plain-language brief: an HTTP JSON service over a data store, with a role model (at least two roles), at least one scheduled or time-dependent behaviour, at least one reporting query, one migration-sensitive data rule, and about 20 use cases; planned size when built 2 to 5 kLOC. **Stack fixed by the brief** (language, framework, database, test runner), the same text for every arm.

**Authors and independence.**
- The **brief, the use-case list, the change requests, the traps, the sealed probes and the answer sheet** are written by a person who is not JC, is not a GS person and has not seen the formulas; **the traps and the change list are chosen by a person who doubts GS** (the skeptic of E2E-1 section 3, who may be the same person). The **trap mix** (visible against carried, and whether phase 1 acceptance is co-primary) is set jointly by the skeptic and the K practitioner, within the ranges of section 4 below, before CMP-0. The GS side holds a logged veto only for ambiguity; the skeptic decides it; vetoed items run anyway and are reported as a sensitivity. The K practitioner never sees the change list, traps or probes; neither does the B prompt author. The G side never sees them before the freeze.
- Any model assistance used in authoring the brief, probes or traps comes from a vendor **not** among the vendors of section 6 (or is logged per item).
- **Machinery reused, not content:** the Pastura pattern (locked scaffold, sealed convention-tolerant oracle, cold canary, arm runners), the P4 trap pipeline and E2E-1's phase structure. The Pastura domain itself is not reused.
- **Locked scaffold.** A starting repository (identical bytes in all arms, hashed) fixing only what a black-box oracle needs: how to install, start and stop the service, the port, a health endpoint, the directory for tests, and the JSON error convention.
- **Oracle.** Black-box probes at the HTTP surface, sealed and hashed outside every repository before any run, never visible to any arm, convention-tolerant (status-class tolerance plus value checks). Validation before freezing: a reference implementation (must pass 100%), stubs (must fail), generic mutants (must kill at least `[90]`%), and, **for each trap, two reference variants: the natural violating implementation, which must fail exactly that trap's rule probes, and a variant with the feature absent, which must fail that trap's feature probes** (so that a rule probe cannot pass vacuously when the feature was never built). Probes are about `[60 to 100]` in total, four or more per use case; 40 phase-2 non-trap probes are simulated. Both variants are logged as hashed fixtures.
- **Answer sheet mapping.** Each answer-sheet entry is mapped, before freezing, to the probes it touches; the mapping is hashed (section 5).
- **Canary.** A cold run per model asks for the product's conventions and behaviours using items with near-zero chance of being guessed (invented identifiers, odd values, an unusual rule), scored by a written rubric against a decoy brief's chance baseline; recall above that baseline on the invented project is INVALID-DESIGN for that model.

**Phase 1, build.** Each arm builds the project from the brief in the way of its row in section 3. Phase 1 is also the **setup cost** of K and G (their substrate and artifacts are created here, with the project). Phase 1 is a ceiling and floor check and a cost measurement; its probes (P-ACC1, section 7) are co-reported and carry no verdict alone, because a complete brief on a small project will probably saturate it (AX2, MX).

**Phase 2, change.** Ten change requests, in a fixed order, each a fresh conversation, applied **cumulatively** to the repository of the run (so carry-over and drift can occur, as in E2E-1), one attempt each, no retry:

| Kind | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Trap, rule visible (T-V)** | 3 | the natural implementation breaks a rule stated in the brief (every arm can see it) |
| **Trap, rule carried (T-C)** | 3 | the natural implementation breaks a rule that an earlier change request in phase 2 introduced; a fresh session must carry it through the repository (this is where persistence can matter) |
| **Specification change** | 2 | a request that supersedes earlier behaviour; probes check the new behaviour and that the old is gone |
| **Reversal** | 1 | removes a feature built in phase 1 |
| **Plain feature** | 1 | a position control with no trap |

The trap count (6) is the simulated value; the skeptic and practitioner may raise it to 8 or 10 (by adding trap steps, not by dropping other kinds) on the evidence of the section 10 sketch, before CMP-0. Each trap is classified before freezing by two raters (the author and the skeptic) from the brief and a black-box run, as visible, implicit-in-code or carried; the **raw agreement table** is reported (kappa on this few items is uninterpretable); disagreements count as the harder class. Results are reported by class. **Scored state:** the tree at `HEAD` of `main` when the conversation ends on its own or hits the cap; after each conversation an arm-neutral script moves work to `main` if the agent left `HEAD` elsewhere, by fast-forward only; work that cannot be fast-forwarded is scored as absent, and the count per arm is reported. After each step the harness runs the sealed probes **outside the repository**; **results are never shown to any arm**.

## 5. The scripted human (operator) and the answer sheet

Spec-kit and GS both assume a human at certain points. A real review is not simulated (that would test the reviewer, not the tool), so each such point is handled by a **registered, scripted operator**, the same engine for every arm, with arm-specific policy text written by the arm's own side and reviewed by the other side (K's policy by the independent practitioner; G's replies drafted from the formulas, reviewed by the K practitioner for faithfulness to the formula; B has no policy). The K practitioner also reviews the harness code that drives K (turn-end detection, skill or template expansion, the "Converged" detector) and the G driver's faithful sending of formula steps. Rules:

1. **The agent asks a question** (any arm): the operator answers from the **answer sheet**, a document written by the brief's author listing the intended answer to each ambiguity of the brief (anticipated from the brief, no knowledge of any arm's questions); a question outside the sheet gets one fixed sentence ("Use your best judgment from the brief."). The number of questions per arm is logged, and **the probes covered by answers each arm received are reported per arm, with a sensitivity restricted to probes that no arm received an answer for**, because the answer sheet is an information channel toward the oracle that favours whichever arm asks most.
2. **The agent's turn ends without a tool call** mid-method: the operator sends the next step of the method (K: the next skill; G: the next formula step) or, for B, nothing more. For a conversation that has not produced a scored state the operator may send "continue" at most `[3]` times, identically in every arm.
3. **Review points, symmetric rule: act on what the method's own check reports, once.** K: when analyze or the checklist report findings, the operator replies once "Fix these findings in the spec, plan and tasks, then re-run the check" (capped at one cycle); when implement asks about unchecked items, "Resolve or record each unchecked item, then continue"; when a check finds nothing, "Continue". G: when a gate, check or formula verification step reports a failure, once "Fix it and re-run the check"; at the formulas' STOP for ratification, "Approved. Continue." K's practitioner may propose an alternative policy; **CMP-0 runs both the practitioner's and this default, and the practitioner (not the outcome) attests which is the competent use of the tool**; the attested one is registered. This is a **registered departure from real use**: ratification is a human act in both methods and is replaced here by approval-by-script, so the pilot measures the tools' machine side; the cost and quality of real human review do not enter the numbers. The count of review points per arm is a descriptive outcome (review surface, section 7).
4. The operator never reads sealed results and never adapts to the arm's progress.

## 6. Models, vendors, harness, metering

**Two vendors**, two different companies, one modern frontier model each, dated snapshot ids `[AT FREEZE]`, CLI and runtime versions pinned and logged, all runs in a short window, arms interleaved in seeded blocks. Candidate default: one Claude and one OpenAI coding model. **The formulas were developed with Claude, so the Claude cell is the "home" cell of arm G and is shown apart from the other.** A third vendor (Gemini by default, whichever is not a builder) is reserved for the judges (section 8).

**Harness.** Each vendor in its own coding-agent CLI (the way spec-kit's integrations and the formulas are meant to be used), with all three arms in the same CLI for that vendor, so the CLI is constant across the contrast within a vendor (it is not constant across vendors; vendor and CLI are one factor, declared). Fresh configuration directory per run, auto-memory off, no user instruction files, no MCP, no web, no resume between conversations, a unique repository path, the planted-memory test of SDX-0 V13. An instruction file that an arm writes into its own repository and the CLI auto-loads is part of the arm (as in E2E-1 rule 1).

**Metering is a hard requirement.** Token counts by category (uncached input, cache write, cache read, output) come from the CLI's own usage output or the vendor's usage log per run; dollars at list prices pinned at freeze. A vendor whose CLI cannot expose token counts is excluded from the cost outcome, and the pilot is not frozen with fewer than two metered vendors. The spec-kit default integration (GitHub Copilot) is not used for measured runs because it does not expose token counts.

## 7. Outcomes

None of the primary outcomes is a property that an arm's own method targets: no arm is told about the hidden probes, no gate of either tool computes them, and no outcome is the output of a tool's own checker.

**Co-primary quality outcomes** (percentage points; descriptive intervals 95%, label intervals Bonferroni-adjusted to 98.33% over the three contrasts, section 12):

- **P-ACC2.** Share of the sealed phase-2 probes **that are not trap-rule probes** (the feature, supersession and reversal probes of the ten steps) passing at `HEAD` of `main` at the end of phase 2, each of the ten steps weighted equally. Mechanical. Trap-rule probes are not in P-ACC2, so the two co-primaries do not share items.
- **P-TRAP.** Over the 6 trap steps, the share **not handled correctly**, intention to treat: a trap step is *correct* only if its feature probes and its rule probes passed at its own step **and** still pass at the end of phase 2; *violated* if the feature is present at the end and a rule probe fails at its step or at the end; *not delivered* if its feature probes fail at the end. A carried trap whose introducing step failed is counted not correct and flagged. **All three categories are reported separately** (descriptive); P-TRAP is the share that is not correct. The share of delivered traps that are violated (P-VIOL, "escaped defects" proper) is co-reported with its own denominator and is secondary.

**Co-reported, no verdict alone:** **P-ACC1**, the share of phase-1 probes passing at the end of phase 2 (regression and ceiling check); T-V and T-C separately; the share of failures that escaped the arm's own net (a probe failing at the end while the arm's own test command exited 0 at its last commit; hooks and gates reported separately where they exist).

**Cost (model dollars; verdict power only in the composite codes of section 13):**
- Tokens (by category, never summed) and dollars per run, split into **setup** (phase 1 and every substrate-creation session), **change work** (phase 2) and **upkeep**, defined by a registered attribution rule: the output tokens of each edit or write tool call are assigned to the path class of its target; the input tokens of each file-read result are assigned to the path class read; tokens that fit neither (reasoning, shell, chat) are reported as *unattributed*. Path classes: production source, tests, docs and specs, tool and substrate directories (such as `.specify`, `specs` and the GS files), configuration; the same rules for all arms; the algorithm is validated on a CMP-0 transcript with a hashed worked example. Upkeep = phase 2 tokens attributed to non-production classes.
- **Cost per accepted change** = (sum of phase 2 dollars) divided by (sum of accepted steps) **per cell** (ratio of sums, interval by bootstrap over runs); an accepted step is one whose own step probes all pass; a cell with no accepted step is not estimable. Shown with setup amortized over the ten steps and unamortized, and at amortization horizons 12, 100 and 1,000 changes with **no verdict on the horizon** (the crossover is arithmetic, not a finding).
- **Human time is descriptive.** An estimate of the hours a typical user of each tool would spend on its setup and its operator points (constitution and policy text for K; bracket filling and answers for G), by task category, in a separate table; **experiment overhead (attestation, review, harness hours) is excluded; FX-1 revision hours are listed as G's preparation**; valued at one rate `[AT FREEZE]`. **The cost classes of section 13 use model dollars only.**

**Review surface (descriptive, one rule for all arms).** Per accepted step: the diff tokens of every changed file, plus the full tokens of every file created in the step, assigned to the path classes above by the same classifier; plus the count of review points and questions the operator handled. The production-code share of this surface is H4's quantity. No per-arm document list is used. This script is separate from the GS review-surface tool (`REVIEW-SURFACE-SPEC.md`), which is the treatment's own instrument and is not used.

**Secondary, 95% intervals, "suggests" wording, no verdict:** frozen independent static analysis (complexity, duplication, dependency cycles, a default lint profile that no arm's prompt or substrate refers to); first-pass completion and cap hits per arm; tool-method failures (spec-kit never reaching "Converged", a formula not reaching its last step), reported as a process-fidelity result per arm; turns and wall-clock; emergent-substrate detector counts (ids, ledgers, instruction files, hooks and ratchets in the final repository of B and K); gate false-blocks and circumvention events (test deletion or weakening, hook edits, baseline edits).

**Reported but never used in a verdict, because they are the arms' own vocabulary:** spec-kit's "Converged" status, checklist completion, constitution compliance as judged by its analyze step; GS's seven-property rubric, the Decagon and SAVED scores, `gs-check` status, the FX-1 conformance checker. They appear in a table labelled "own-vocabulary indicators, not outcomes", used only as manipulation checks and for the process-fidelity result.

## 8. Blinding and judges

Acceptance is mechanical. Judged outcomes (secondary): (1) whether a diff implements the change in a way the sealed probes cannot see, to detect gaming such as special-casing (a 10% sample of accepted steps per arm); (2) pairwise maintainability of the final trees of the same vendor and run index across arms. **Judges are from a third vendor** (not either builder), fresh stateless sessions that see only the artifacts, **blind to the arm label**, with tool directories and substrate files stripped (names, comments and commit style still leak; stated; a blinding-guess test, each judge guesses the arm and the rate is reported), **calibrated first on a planted-flaw set** with kappa reported; a judge that fails calibration is replaced or the judged outcomes are declared unevaluable. The assistant that wrote this protocol and the GS-side people do not judge. JC does not read blind pairs (he is the stakeholder); the skeptic reads a sample of 30 diffs with substrate paths stripped, reported as the only human reading. The judged outcomes cannot change a class or a composite code.

## 9. Controls, floor and ceiling, and the pilot CMP-0

- **Positive control.** One extra step per run, **outside the ten, run last on a throwaway copy of the final tree so that it does not feed forward**, whose trap rule is stated in the change text itself. The count is **pooled per arm across the two vendors**: each arm must handle it correctly in at least 8 of its 10 runs, or the trap measure is declared not working for that arm and that arm's P-TRAP rows carry the label "premise failed". P-TRAP is always reported, with the label attached, never suppressed. The consequence is per arm and only that (it is not an INVALID-DESIGN trigger of the whole pilot). A discoverable-rule item (the rule is visible in code comments at the start of phase 2) follows the same logic.
- **Drift check (replaces revision 1's A/A "noise" test).** A duplicated set of the bare arm (B-b, `[3]` runs per vendor), interleaved in time with the main runs and **not pooled** into B. If B and B-b differ by at least 2 x Delta on a co-primary in the same direction in both vendors, that is a drift signature (time, order or harness): the cause is investigated, and if it is a harness cause the affected outcome is INVALID-DESIGN for that vendor; otherwise it is reported as the noise estimate. The family-wise false-trigger rate of this rule is computed in the simulation before freezing (precondition 17.8). B-b adds to the cost; dropping it and spending the runs on more traps or models is an option for JC (section 10).
- **Oracle validation.** Reference implementation, stubs, mutants, and the two per-trap variants (section 4); a probe that fails the reference is removed before freezing; no probe changes after.
- **Manipulation checks.** K: stock spec-kit, files created per step, completion of each documented step, "Converged" reached or the cap hit; G: the FX-1 conformance checker on the final repository and the formulas' own verification step, and the per-CLI enforcement test of section 3; B: the emergent-substrate detector. A run whose arm did not perform its method is **not** excluded (a user following the method would have met the same); it is scored as is and counted in the process-fidelity result. Infrastructure faults (outage, rate limit, process killed by the harness) are re-run once in the same slot, the original archived; infrastructure loss above 5% found before data is a stop-and-amend, after data it is INVALID-DESIGN for that cell. **Fidelity attestation of the main runs:** before any probe result is unsealed, the K practitioner reads at least two K transcripts per vendor (random draw) and signs that they are a competent use of the tool; the same for G by the reviewer of the G driver, who checks the formula steps were sent faithfully.
- **Floor and ceiling pilot CMP-0** (data excluded from the pilot's analysis). **Both builder vendors**, a **sibling fixture** (same machinery, a different invented domain by the same independent author, smaller: about 10 use cases and 5 change requests, 2 traps visible and 1 carried), three arms, 2 runs per arm per vendor, 12 runs. It is a **feasibility, fidelity and headroom pilot**, not a source of variance estimates for the registered simulation (two runs per cell give one degree of freedom per SD, on a different fixture; the registered simulation keeps its assumptions, with a sensitivity grid over run SD 0.3 to 1.0 logit, and CMP-0's SDs are reported beside it, labelled rough). It checks: (a) **headroom**: B's P-ACC2 at the end of phase 2 lies between 30% and 85%; above 85% (ceiling) the CMP-1 fixture's **reserve traps** (a pool of at least 6 further trap steps authored by the skeptic and hashed before CMP-0, difficulty rated by the skeptic before any run) replace trap steps **drawn at random**, never chosen by any arm's failures, and never from CMP-0 outcomes of individual items; if no headroom can be built the pilot is declared not worth running; below 30% (floor) easier items are drawn from the same reserve by the same rule; (b) cost per run, per-arm caps and cap-hit share (above); (c) headless invocation and command resolution of spec-kit's steps on both CLIs, and G's per-CLI enforcement test (section 3); (d) whether the operator policies are executable without human help, and the K policy comparison of section 5.3; (e) the preamble's effect (first skill or step with and without it); (f) the attribution algorithm worked example (section 7); (g) a fidelity attestation by the K practitioner of the K transcripts, listing anything the harness prevented. Nothing in a formula or in K's package is changed because of what the contrast looks like; changes are allowed only for harness faults and for items (c), (d), (e) and (g), and are logged. Go/no-go uses feasibility and the variance condition of section 12 item 6, never a point estimate of a difference.

## 10. Sample size, power sketch and what k = 5 can and cannot show

Plan: 2 vendors x 3 arms x 5 runs = **30 runs**, plus 6 drift-check runs (B-b) = 36, plus the positive-control step inside each run. Units of replication: the run (a full build plus ten changes), one task, so **inference is conditional on this task and these two vendors**; runs within a vendor are repeated draws of the same stochastic process on the same fixture, not independent tasks, and more runs per vendor do not make the result more general (only more models and more tasks do).

**Simulation** (`CMP-1-sim\simulate.py`, revision 2; every effect and noise value is an assumption; 4,000 simulated pilots per row; descriptive 95% and label 98.33% Welch-Satterthwaite intervals on the vendor-stratified mean difference; infeasible rows such as an 85% comparator plus 20 points are not printed; the revision 1 rule that required the point estimate to reach Delta is dropped because it capped power near one half). Run SD 0.5 logit for P-ACC2, 0.7 for P-TRAP, vendor SD 0.4, vendor-by-arm SD 0.3. At k = 5 per vendor and arm (`results.md` section 1):

- **P-ACC2, comparator at 55%.** P(AHEAD) for a true gain of 10 points is **0.22**, 15 points 0.37, **20 points 0.60**, 30 points 0.90; the 95% half-width is about **12 points**. The pilot can see a gain of 20 points or more on this outcome and will usually miss 10. Near the ceiling (comparator 85%, true gain 10) P(AHEAD) is 0.59 and the half-width about 7 points; headroom is what the pilot lacks there.
- **P-TRAP (6 trap steps, comparator not correct in 50%).** Half-width about **23 points**; P(AHEAD) for a true 20-point reduction is only **0.21** (0.34 if the comparator fails 30%), for 30 points 0.48 (0.77). Six traps is a coarse instrument: **the pilot is weak on the outcome on which the GS thesis lives**, and a non-detection there means "not resolved", not "no difference". More trap steps per run is the cheapest repair (each is a fresh session); the skeptic and practitioner may raise 6 to 8 or 10 before CMP-0.
- **Cost, run SD 0.30 in logs.** A true cost ratio of 2.0 is called COSTLIER with probability **0.93**, 1.5 with **0.58**, 1.25 only 0.24; half-width about 1.33-fold. Cost is where the pilot can speak with some confidence.
- **Sensitivity to the run SD** (true 20-point gain, k = 5): P(AHEAD) on P-ACC2 is 0.73 at SD 0.3, 0.60 at 0.5, 0.43 at 0.7, 0.25 at 1.0; on P-TRAP 0.31, 0.25, 0.20, 0.14. If CMP-0 suggests SDs near 1.0 the pilot is largely uninformative on quality.
- **Ties.** The probability of certifying TIE at k = 5 is 0.00 at mid-range scores and 0.11 near the ceiling even when the arms are truly equal. A pilot cannot establish that two tools are equivalent; "no difference seen" is the only wording it supports.
- **Falsifiers** (section 2): H1, H2 and H3 fire with probability 0.34, 0.48 and 0.39 when their priors are false: all three are weak at k = 5.
- **False labels.** With no true quality difference and equal costs (scenario S0), the chance that at least one contrast carries an AHEAD label is **0.14** and a BEHIND label **0.12**; a "GS scored higher" sentence would be licensed with probability 0.10 by chance alone. These numbers are printed beside every public sentence (section 14).
- **Composite codes** (`results.md` section 2, probabilities over simulated pilots). With no quality difference and the cost prior (K/B 3, G/B 2): O9 (a tool costlier, quality unresolved) 0.76, O3 (bare holds) 0.56, O1 0.09, O2 0.11, O8 (all inconclusive) 0.18. If G truly cuts trap failures by 20 points against both comparators (S1): O4 (G ahead) only **0.36**, O9 0.55, O1 0.06, O2 0.27 (K behind G, a true loss for K in this scenario; the same bar applies to both tools). If both tools beat B by 20 points on both outcomes (S3): O4 and O5 each 0.68. If K truly beats G and B on trap failures by 20 (S4): O1 0.26, O5 0.35. The pilot will therefore usually return O9 or O8, "costlier, quality unresolved" or "nothing resolved", unless an effect is large; that is the honest expected result, and it is the reason the rule of section 12 item 6 decides the next step on variances, not on estimates.
- **Interval check.** With no vendor-by-arm interaction and no true difference the 95% interval excludes zero in 0.044 of simulations; with interaction SD 0.3 it covers the conditional truth (the mean of the two vendor-specific differences) in 0.959. The intervals are about these two vendors.

**What k = 5 can and cannot show.** It can show a large difference (about 20 points or more on P-ACC2, about 30 on P-TRAP; about 1.5 times or more on cost), a failure of a method to complete, and the size of the noise. It cannot show a difference smaller than about 12 points on acceptance or 23 on traps, cannot certify a tie, cannot estimate vendor dependence (two vendors), cannot generalize beyond this task and these two models, and cannot apportion a difference among a tool's components. **The result is labelled "pilot, one task, two models, 5 runs per arm and vendor, estimates only" wherever it appears.** The sketch is recomputed with the pilot's registered trap count and the CMP-0 SD sensitivity before freezing (17.8).

**Alternative allocations offered to JC (the same money, a different question).** (1) *Checkpointed trap episodes:* from each arm's own phase 1 build, branch about 20 independent trap episodes (carried traps start from a checkpoint taken after the rule-introducing step, verified by probes), analysed with the build as the cluster: about six times the trap observations per dollar and the cascade separated from the trap, at the price of dropping the cumulative path except as a descriptive drift check. (2) *Models over runs:* five or six models from at least three vendors, 3 arms, 2 runs each, with a model random effect: makes vendor dependence estimable and the intervals about a population of models. (3) *Mechanism ablation (a CMP-1b):* G, G with its executed checks disabled (same formula text), and K plus one community enforcement extension chosen by the practitioner, with a small B as the floor; it tests the stated GS hypothesis directly and answers threat 6. The default plan is the three-arm pilot JC approved; these are for the next decision.

## 12. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Per cell (vendor, arm): the mean of each outcome over the 5 runs and its between-run SD. Pooled: the equal-weight mean over the two vendors. Contrasts: G-B, K-B, G-K (and ratios for cost). **Scales:** quality outcomes in percentage points, cost as the log ratio.
2. **Intervals.** Descriptive: 95% Welch-Satterthwaite on the vendor-stratified difference (as in the sketch), with a stratified percentile bootstrap over runs within vendor and arm as a sensitivity, especially for P-TRAP (values 0 to 6 of 6). **Labels** (AHEAD, BEHIND, TIE, SUGGESTED) are assigned from the Bonferroni-adjusted 98.33% interval (three contrasts per outcome); the per-vendor 95% intervals are used for VENDOR-DEPENDENT. A cell pair in which both arms have zero variance in every vendor cell is INCONCLUSIVE, never TIE. No further multiplicity adjustment: six quality contrasts and three cost ratios are declared, and the claims are "estimates"; the family-wise false-label rate under the null is printed with every public sentence.
3. The class chain of section 13 is an ordered if-then-else in a hashed script; the script is tested by enumerating all class combinations to show that the composite codes are exhaustive (the code O0 catches the rest) before freezing.
4. Sensitivities (descriptive; none changes a class): intention-to-treat variants; first-pass only; the Claude "home" cell and the other cell apart; without setup; with and without human time; price scenarios (plus or minus 50%, cache reads at 10%); excluding vetoed items; excluding runs where an arm did not complete its method; excluding cap-hit steps; the answer-sheet sensitivity of section 5.1.
5. No hypothesis test is registered. Outcome data are never used to change an arm, a probe or a threshold; after data, only the deviations log changes (protocol section 5).
6. **Go/no-go for the full study (registered, variance-based, independent of the estimated differences).** Proceed to the larger study (more models, more trap steps, the K+ arm, a second task family) if the observed per-arm run SDs of P-ACC2 and P-TRAP, entered into the simulation, give P(AHEAD) of at least 0.8 for a true 20-point difference at some k of at most `[10]` per vendor and arm within a budget of `[AT FREEZE]` dollars; otherwise do not proceed and publish that decision with the SDs. The decision and its numbers are published whatever the estimates are. An INVALID-DESIGN outcome stops the line (the design is redone, not enlarged).

## 13. Decision table (registered; mechanical)

**Axis 0, INVALID-DESIGN** (fix and register a linked CMP-1b; no comparison statement for the affected part): canary recall above the decoy baseline on either model; harness persistence found; oracle fails reference, per-trap variants or mutant validation; bare arm's P-ACC2 below 30% or above 85% in a vendor cell (that cell's quality rows are unevaluable; cost is still reported; if more than half of the cells, the quality rows of the pilot are unevaluable); a harness-caused drift signature (section 9); fewer than two metered vendors; vendor alias, model or CLI changed mid-window; **K's installed version or template hash differs from its pin in a run**; the G formula tag differs from its pin in a run; independence of the authors or the K practitioner not met; a third-vendor judge unavailable (judged outcomes unevaluable only); infrastructure loss above 5% found after data. Per-arm labels, not pilot-wide triggers: "premise failed" (positive control), "enforcement absent" (a CLI in which G's executed elements do not fire).

**Class of a contrast X-Y on a quality outcome** (P-ACC2, or P-TRAP read so that a positive difference favours X). Let [lo, hi] be the 98.33% interval of the pooled difference d, "vendors agree" mean both vendor differences have the sign of d, and Delta = 10 points. **Assigned by the first row that applies, in this order:**

| Order | Class | Condition | May be said, with "in this pilot" |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | INCONCLUSIVE (zero variance) | both arms have zero variance in every vendor cell | report; never TIE |
| 1 | VENDOR-DEPENDENT | the two per-vendor 95% intervals are disjoint | report both cells; no pooled statement |
| 2 | X AHEAD | lo > 0 and the vendors agree | "X scored higher than Y by [d, interval] on this measure, on this task, with these two models." |
| 2 | Y AHEAD (X BEHIND) | hi < 0 and the vendors agree | the same with X and Y swapped |
| 3 | TIE | -Delta < lo and hi < Delta | "The two differed by less than Delta points here." Never "equivalent". |
| 4 | X SUGGESTED / Y SUGGESTED | the interval includes 0, |d| >= Delta and the vendors agree | "A difference of about d points favouring X was seen but the interval includes zero; the pilot cannot tell." Worth including in the full study. |
| 5 | INCONCLUSIVE | otherwise | report the interval; state what k or traps would resolve it |

How large d is relative to Delta is reported descriptively beside AHEAD and BEHIND (the revision 1 requirement that d reach Delta is dropped).

**Cost, per pair (model dollars, label interval of the log ratio).** CHEAPER (entirely below 1), COSTLIER (entirely above 1), otherwise SIMILAR if inside 0.8 to 1.25, else INCONCLUSIVE; for total, setup, change work, upkeep and per accepted change, and the crossover arithmetic. **Review surface**: reported as differences in tokens and in the production-code share with 95% intervals; no class.

**Composite codes** (T a tool, Y a comparator; BEHIND(T, Y) = Y AHEAD of T on at least one quality outcome; AHEAD(T, Y) = T AHEAD of Y on at least one quality outcome and BEHIND(T, Y) on neither). **No evaluation order: all codes that apply are reported.**

| Code | Condition |
|---|---|
| **O1** | BEHIND(G, K) or BEHIND(G, B), or G COSTLIER than K (or B) while both quality outcomes of that pair are TIE |
| **O2** | the same with K as the tool and G or B as the comparator |
| **O3** | no tool is AHEAD or SUGGESTED against B on either quality outcome, and B is CHEAPER than both tools |
| **O4 / O5** | AHEAD(G, K) or AHEAD(G, B) / AHEAD(K, G) or AHEAD(K, B) |
| **O6** | a pair is TIE on both quality outcomes |
| **O7** | at least one AHEAD, BEHIND, SUGGESTED or VENDOR-DEPENDENT cell, and none of O1 to O6 applies (including trade-offs: a tool ahead on quality and COSTLIER) |
| **O8** | all six quality cells are INCONCLUSIVE |
| **O9** | a tool is COSTLIER than a comparator and no quality cell for that pair is AHEAD or BEHIND (quality unresolved); stated for each tool; not a loss |
| **O0** | none of the above (residual: "no label"; the intervals are reported) |

Public sentences print the pilot's simulated probability of the code under the global null, and the pilot's power for a 20-point difference next to any "without detected gain" statement (section 14).

## 15. Threats to validity (declared), author bias first

1. **Author bias: the GS side wrote this comparison, chose the comparator, the metric family and the decision wording, and will analyse the data.** The pilot's tier is therefore at most **B** (in-repo tag pushed before data; **A** only if an external record predates the first run and the independence conditions of the roles hold) and the result is called an **author-side comparison** in every public sentence. Mitigations, each a registered obligation and none a cure: (a) the brief, probes, traps, change list and answer sheet are authored by independent persons, the traps by a skeptic; (b) the K arm's setup is written or reviewed by an independent spec-kit user and attested, the path chosen by that user with no GS default, and the K driver code reviewed by that user; (c) the G arm gets **no tuning beyond the frozen formula**, FX-1 uses disjoint fixtures, and FX-1's hours are counted; (d) the primary metrics are hidden behavioural probes and trap handling, which no arm targets, and the own-vocabulary indicators are barred from verdicts; (e) the unfavourable outcomes are written first and mirrored at the same evidential bar (O1 and O2); (f) judges are from a third vendor, stateless, blind, calibrated; (g) the analysis scripts, classifier, operator policies, answer-sheet mapping and decision rules are hashed before data; (h) all runs, transcripts and failures are published; (i) the spec-kit maintainers get a pre-publication courtesy read with a binding rule for corrections; (j) the comparison is **offered for replication by someone not on the GS side**, with the full package, as a condition of calling any result more than a pilot; (k) CMP-0 is excluded and cannot tune the contrast; (l) the go/no-go rule is variance-based and registered.
2. **One task, one fixture, small.** At 2 to 5 kLOC a bare frontier model may need no specification discipline, which would favour B; at larger scale or longer horizons the ordering may change. The trap mix, the fresh-conversation unit and the demotion of phase 1 are design choices that decide where an effect can appear; the skeptic and the K practitioner share them (section 4), but the task still asks the question the GS thesis asks. A spec-kit-side hypothesis (H6) is registered for balance.
3. **Two vendors, and vendor and CLI are one factor.** The formulas were developed with Claude (home cell). The pooled number is conditional on these two; vendor dependence cannot be estimated.
4. **Scripted human.** Both methods lean on a human reviewer; ratification is scripted (section 5.3). The pilot measures neither the value of real review nor its cost. The remediation default for K (act on findings once) keeps K's checks from being made inert, but it remains a script.
5. **Phase 2 is cumulative and one attempt.** One early failure can cascade into later steps; trap outcomes are not independent within a run. Reported as run-level outcomes; the intention-to-treat definition of P-TRAP counts cascaded failures; the sketch does not model the cascade, so its half-widths are optimistic.
6. **Spec-kit "stock" is not the strongest spec-kit.** The enforcement add-ons in its community catalogue (deterministic hooks, drift validation) are outside the pilot, and they are the part of the ecosystem closest to GS's claim. An optional fourth arm (K+) is **not added by default**; alternative (3) of section 10 is the way to add it, and public wording says "stock".
7. **Bare is a floor, not an expert.** A steelman prompt (E2E-1 arm X) is absent; a bare win does not say that no method helps.
8. **Memorization of spec-kit and its templates** by the models (public and widely copied) helps K and B; the GS formulas are less likely to be in training data. Direction of net bias unknown.
9. **Models, CLIs, prices and tool releases move.** The result is dated; a repeat in six months may read differently.
10. **The formulas have not run in a registered run.** If FX-1 shows a formula fails, CMP-1 would measure a broken G; hence the precondition.
11. **Noise at k = 5** is large and its structure is assumed until measured.
12. **The answer sheet is a channel from the brief's author to the oracle** (section 5.1); mitigated by the mapping and the restricted-probe sensitivity, not removed.
13. **Critics are of one family** (section 18).
