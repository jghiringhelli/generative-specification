## 1. Question, and what it does and does not answer

Given a fixed, reviewed specification package with numbered acceptance criteria, how fast and at what cost does a coding agent under each regime reach each stage's machine-checked completeness criteria, up to a **verified COMPLETE** build, where "reached" is decided by a frozen scorer and never by the author or the agent; how often do claims of completion run ahead of the scorer; how many defects escape after COMPLETE is declared; and **at which stage, if any, does the substrate's cumulative cost, with setup and upkeep inside, fall below the comparators?**

It is the second of the two translations (spec to code and pipeline; the first, intent to spec, is outside). It is **not** a head-start claim (the substrate has setup and upkeep cost, counted); not a claim about change over time (SDX-1 and SDX-8); not a claim that the spec was cheap to produce (the spec is an input; its production cost is outside and reported as not measured); not a claim about human developers (model-only first); not a claim about specs that are not already in numbered-criteria form (section 12).

Honest prior, written before any data (protocol guard f; **DRAFT by the assistant, JC must confirm or overwrite before any data**): the substrate is slower or no faster than the expert prompt at S0 and S1 (H9-c, a registered risk: Gloaguen and others report more than 20% extra cost from context files without a success gain); a crossover is no earlier than S2, modal at S3; and "no crossover by COMPLETE within the cap" is a live outcome, probability near 35% **as a statement about the world, and much higher as a statement about what the registered procedure will report** (section 2a, "Power").

Intuition in falsifiable form: "Building a fixed reviewed spec from an empty repository with a mid-tier coding agent, an enforced substrate reaches verified COMPLETE at least 15% cheaper than an expert prompt and than the same substrate with advisory gates." One falsifier list is used everywhere in this file: **EQUIVALENT, COSTLIER or CHEAPER-SMALL on H9-1 contradicts this version of the intuition (REFUTED for this version); INCONCLUSIVE leaves it open (state the n that would resolve it).**

## 2. Hypotheses, readouts, falsifiers, controls

### 2.1 Readouts, defined once

Unit: one chain = one build from an empty repository plus the locked scaffold and the spec package, run by a stateless harness that opens fresh sessions (only the repository carries state, the SDX-1 rule) until the scorer reports S4 verified (plus one confirmation session, section 4.4) or the cap is hit. After every session the harness snapshots the working tree to a separate ref with hooks disabled (a **checkpoint**) and the scorer runs on a clean clone. The identical task prompt of every arm says "commit your work before ending the session", so each arm's own hooks execute on the agent's commits. (SDX-1 section 3 has the harness commit; any change SDX-1's critics make to that is routed to SDX-1's adjudication, section 13.)

- **Cost:** all model spend of every session and attempt, dollars from a price table registered at freeze, tokens second, agent wall-clock third; a **cache-neutral sensitivity table** (cache reads charged at the uncached input rate); a cost verdict row applies only if the dollar result and the cache-neutral result fall in the same class family, otherwise qualifier Q5. **View V1 (registered, verdict):** all in-chain spend including bootstrap and upkeep of the arm's own artifacts, plus **Z_project** = the median model spend of three registered regeneration runs, executed by the harness at freeze, of a fixed generator script and model that rebuilds the arm's project-specific files from the spec package (C3 and C4: the ledger and sentinel generation; other arms zero), with the file classification (project-specific charged, generic not) registered and reviewed by the independent reviewer. **V2 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 minus the tokens the harness classifies, by file path, as writing or maintaining substrate files (setup and upkeep removed; expected favourable to C3 and C4). **V3 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 plus every arm's authoring hours and the human review hours of project-specific files at $75 per hour, charged once per chain. Row T9 compares V1 with V2.
- **Stage verified time.** A stage k is **verified at checkpoint j** when its checks (section 4.3) pass at j **and at the next M = 2 checkpoints** (non-terminal stickiness: late events cannot reach back and re-price early stages), or, for S4, at j and at the confirmation checkpoint. The cost to verified stage k is the cumulative cost through checkpoint j. Later regressions of a verified stage are **counted per stage as regression events** (reported, with the cost of the repair sessions, never silently re-pricing earlier stages). Stages are cumulative: verified S(k+1) requires verified S(0..k).
- **COMPLETE** = S4 verified and the variant-failure rate E_var on the held-out suite at or below the band delta_v of section 4.5. A chain that verifies S4 but has E_var above delta_v is a **premature COMPLETE** and counts as not complete.
- **Cap and censoring (one rule).** A single **dollar cap** of 3 times B_4 for every arm, plus an infrastructure session limit of 60 sessions. **Every chain that does not reach a stage (or COMPLETE) enters the analysis of that stage at log(cap), including chains censored by either limit and premature COMPLETE chains.** Sensitivities: actual cost at censoring; a rank-based analysis with non-completers tied at the worst rank. The completion rate (share of chains reaching COMPLETE) is reported per arm as a secondary rate with its own class.
- **Claim calibration.** At the end of every session the agent must end its final message with `CLAIM: S0` to `CLAIM: S4` or `CLAIM: NONE` (the same prompt in every arm). Per chain: **over-claim rate** = share of session ends at which the claimed stage exceeds the scorer's non-sticky stage at that checkpoint, `NONE` counted as claiming S(-1); **under-claim rate** = share at which the scorer's stage exceeds the claim. Reported together, labelled **targeted** for C3 and C4 (the substrate's gates couple claiming to blocking, so the metric partly measures its instructed behaviour); chains with fewer than 3 session ends are excluded by a registered rule.
- **Interventions:** scripted product-owner replies used, gate-failure loops, restarts (infrastructure only), harness nudges (one fixed text, in every arm, issued after **three consecutive sessions that produced no new commit**, a scorer-independent signal, at most three per chain; a defined event, not free text).
- **Verified throughput** VT = criteria-weighted verified criteria per dollar and per token at each stage and cumulatively (exploratory).
- **Escapes** after S4: the held-out suite (4.5) and the secondary judge audit (section 7).

**Primary readout (one per trunk):** the cost to verified COMPLETE, per chain, on the log scale, with the single censoring rule above (H9-1).

### 2.2 Classification of any contrast

Delta = mean of log X minus mean of log Y per chain (negative = X cheaper); primary family at 95% intervals (alpha 0.05 over the fixed sequence of section 8, no further correction); secondary rates at 95% with Holm over H9-a and H9-b. **The class is decided by the BCa interval** (10,000 replicates, seed registered); the Monte Carlo permutation p-value (100,000, seed registered) is reported and decides nothing; if they disagree on the class the less favourable class is reported; BCa coverage is checked on pilot-shaped simulated data before freeze with percentile-t as fallback below 0.93. SESOI delta = ln(0.85) in magnitude (15% in cost), provisional, **fixed from the decision value of a saving before the pilot and not movable by it** (a smaller saving is probably not worth a heavy process whose human authoring and upkeep time sits outside model dollars); for rates, 10 percentage points. Classes are a **partition**:

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| CHEAPER-RELEVANT | U < 0 and p <= minus delta |
| CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED | U < 0, p > minus delta, L < minus delta |
| CHEAPER-SMALL | U < 0 and L >= minus delta (a measurable saving below the registered minimum worth having) |
| EQUIVALENT | the interval contains 0 and lies inside minus delta to plus delta (TOST) |
| COSTLIER | L > 0 (COSTLIER-RELEVANT if p >= delta) |
| INCONCLUSIVE | everything else |

**"Positive" means CHEAPER-RELEVANT or CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED, nothing else**; CHEAPER-SMALL licenses no claim that the substrate "reaches COMPLETE cheaper". Order of weakness: COSTLIER, EQUIVALENT, INCONCLUSIVE, CHEAPER-SMALL, CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED, CHEAPER-RELEVANT.

**Power.** Chain endpoint: log cost to COMPLETE (non-completers enter at log cap). The earlier SD grid is withdrawn as too optimistic: censoring at the cap adds a mixture component (non-completers enter about ln 3 above completers), which at 20% censoring puts the SD near 0.4 or more. For a 95% two-sided interval and 80% power for a positive class at a true gap equal to delta (0.163): n per arm = 2 (2.80 x SD / 0.163)^2. SD 0.15 gives 14, 0.20 gives 24, 0.25 gives 37, 0.30 gives 53, **0.40 gives 95, 0.45 gives 120** (SD from the pilot; with 3 chains per arm it is unreliable). Planning n = 20 detects, at 80% power, a gap of about 0.89 SD: 0.22 (20%) at SD 0.25, **0.36 (30%) at SD 0.40**. **Probability that the registered procedure reports a crossover, computed at the central prior:** central ratio 0.90 (|log| 0.105), SD 0.25, n = 20: single-contrast power about 0.26 (z = 1.96 against a standardized gap of 1.33), lower for the intersection-union over C2 and C3 and lower for earlier stages. **At the central prior "no crossover within the cap" would be reported with probability above 0.7 and is consistent with every prior in this table.** Therefore the pilot-informed simulation of section 14 item 2 is mandatory before freeze: if the simulated probability that the registered procedure reports a crossover (central prior, approved n) is below 0.5, **the experiment is bought as an estimation study** (stage-wise cost curves and intervals, no verdict rows beyond the INCONCLUSIVE family for H9-2), with JC's signed acceptance before any chain runs. One blinded variance re-estimation (no arm labels, no contrast) at the interim look can only raise n inside the approved cap; delta cannot be raised after the pilot. Rates: n = 20 detects 10 points only at a baseline near 0.5 with about 40% power; H9-a, H9-b and H9-e are labelled "direction" unless the pilot shows otherwise.

### 2b. Controls and validity gates (guard a)

- **Scorer sensitivity (positive control PC2).** 30 planted mutants of the reference implementation (6 per stage boundary), each with a known stage (the highest stage whose checks it still passes), plus the reference (must score S4 and COMPLETE), degenerate stubs ("always 200", "always 4xx", empty repository: each below S0), and a stage-boundary suite built by deleting one criterion's behaviour at a time. The scorer must assign at least 95% of mutants the registered stage and never a higher one. Otherwise repair before any chain is read; if it cannot be repaired: INVALID-DESIGN.
- **Planted cost control (PC1) C2-pad** (n = 10): C2 plus a registered block of about 30% extra irrelevant context text (fixed, hashed) delivered every session. It must be classified COSTLIER than C2 on cost to COMPLETE at the planned n; otherwise the experiment cannot detect a known cost difference and any EQUIVALENT-type row is INVALID-DESIGN (row T6 is then withheld). This replaces the earlier half-cap dose check, which tests monotonicity of truncation, not sensitivity.
- **Systematic-drift control C2b (A/A):** independent run of C2, n = 10. The point difference between C2 and C2b at least delta with the 95% interval excluding zero signals drift, order or window effects: INVALID-DESIGN. Limit stated: noise is measured for C2 only; per-arm SDs are reported.
- **Negative control C4-stale (n = 20, a direct contrast with C4):** C4 whose criteria-ledger ids and sentinel routing are wrong for a registered fraction (25% of entries), the wrong entries generated by an independent model of another vendor as plausible-but-wrong content (registered seed; a mechanical shuffle only as the fallback). **The content claim requires C4 minus C4-stale positive (H9-d)**; otherwise "true ledger content not shown" (Q2). The share of stale entries the agent repairs is logged per checkpoint; if more than half are repaired by S1 the control is labelled "self-repaired, uninformative".
- **Floor and ceiling, on arm-independent evidence.** C1 fails S0 in every chain: the spec package, scaffold or harness is defective: INVALID-DESIGN. **Pooled censoring above 60% of all chains entering a primary contrast** at COMPLETE: the cap is too low for the primary: INVALID-DESIGN (a higher cap is a new registration). Censoring above 50% of chains in a **comparator or control** arm, applied symmetrically to C2, C3 and C5, is also a trigger for that contrast. C1 reaches COMPLETE in every chain with over-claim rate below 0.05: H9-a and H9-b are not testable (ceiling, flagged); cost readouts stay interpretable. **If only C4 fails S0 in more than 50% of its chains, or censors more than the comparators, that is an outcome (row T7, "the substrate fails on this construct"), analysed under ITT, never invalidity.**
- **Planted-ambiguity probes** accept an admissible set of resolutions registered before data (section 4.2); the channel check requires at least 80% of the planted ambiguities to be asked about by some arm in the pilot, or the channel is repaired.
- **Manipulation checks M1 and M2** inherited from SDX-1-ARMS section 4 for C1 and C2 (A5's text) and applied to C3, C4, C5, C4-stale and C2-pad (no element beyond what the arm defines; C5's hook is a generic gate only). **M2 is re-run on the build-task shape;** if the practitioner supplies a build-specific variant C2-B it replaces C2 as the comparator inside the intersection-union (strength check run on it); otherwise every claim row carries the qualifier "rival prompt not adapted to the task shape".
- **Gate-execution check:** gate executions per session per arm are logged; if fewer than `[FROM SDX-9-P]`% of C4 and C5 sessions execute a gate, "enforced" is withdrawn for that arm (Q7). **Circumvention** (C4 and C5; C3 cannot circumvent a gate that does not block): edited tests or fixtures, `--no-verify`, forged lock hash, edited ledger to satisfy a gate; counts per arm; intention to treat; above `[FROM SDX-9-P]`% "enforced" is withdrawn.
- **Invalid-design triggers** (each tested by "which true world does this label invalid", Lessons E.2; none fires on the treatment arm's own failure): the above; canary recall above zero; model snapshot, CLI or Node changed mid-window, or the registered price table or its application script edited after freeze; harness persistence found; scaffold or spec-package hash mismatch; stage-tagging disagreement between the reviewer and the author above 10% of criteria after settlement; GS vocabulary found in the spec package by the judge; held-out suite found to overlap the visible spec text; practitioner independence rule broken; a cap hit the harness classifies as infrastructure-caused (logged before unblinding). **Cap hits as such are an outcome**, reported per arm, never a trigger.

## 4. Fixture, spec package, stages, margins

### 4.1 The spec package and the fixture: Pastura-full

Pastura (invented, no public corpus, canary run cold per model, recall above zero is INVALID-DESIGN) with the locked scaffold of SDX-1 (Node, TypeScript strict, `node:http`, `node:sqlite`, vitest, fixed paths, hash-checked after every session), the spec package placed read-only in the repository (hash-checked like the scaffold), identical bytes in every arm. **Pastura-full** is defined as: the base spec and the **final-state resolution of SDX-1's ten changes** (the supersession of change 6 applied, the emergency move of change 3 retired by change 9, so the end-state probes are those live at SDX-1's snapshot 10) **plus an extension package E** written by independent authors, covering the criteria SDX-1 does not: validation and error paths, pagination, idempotence, latency and size budgets, the real-runtime classes of S3, and the run-from-clean-clone interface of S4. Size: at least 60 criteria (provisional; final size from SDX-9-P), each with at least one sealed executable probe carrying the criterion id and stage tag; at least 1.5x as many probes as criteria. Six planted ambiguities (two touching S1, two S2, one S3, one S4), each bound to one spec section. **The spec is in numbered-criteria form, the form the substrate consumes natively; every claim row says so (section 12).**

**Stage definitions given to the agent** (in the task prompt): short plain-language versions of the first column of the table in section 4.3, with no thresholds. S4 requires a README containing a fenced block labelled `quickstart` whose commands the scorer extracts and runs in a clean container (the "stranger script").

### 4.2 The question channel and the planted ambiguities

Headless sessions cannot ask mid-session. The channel is the same in every arm: the agent may end a session with up to 5 lines `QUESTION[<spec section id>]: <text>` per session; the harness answers them in the next session's prompt (the scripted answer if the section carries a planted ambiguity, else the fixed sentence "no clarification beyond the text of that section"). Asking is logged, never penalized and **never gates a stage**. Each planted ambiguity has an **admissible set of at least two resolutions consistent with the spec text, registered before data, and checked by the independent reviewer**. Its sealed probe accepts any resolution in the admissible set while no answer has been delivered, and only the scripted resolution once the answer has been delivered. An arm that never asks is therefore not blocked; an arm that asks gains nothing except the avoidance of a wrong guess, and the effect of asking is read from the exploratory asking rate and from the outcomes. (Deviation from P6 section 3, stated: P6's gate (d) required ambiguities to be "asked and answered or recorded"; both critics of the first round showed that this makes asking, an instructed behaviour of the substrate arm, a precondition of the stage, against protocol guard (b). The gate is removed from the scorer.)

### 4.3 Stages and their machine-checkable completeness criteria

Each criterion is tagged to exactly one stage by the written rule of the second column, by the independent reviewer, before data. A stage is verified when its checks hold at the checkpoint and the next M = 2 checkpoints (section 2.1). The thresholds d_k are **pre-declared allowed deviations**, provisional, justified at freeze from SDX-9-P using arm-masked pooled data (SESOI discipline of SDX-1 section 2.2). **All checks are black-box and scorer-side, identical for every arm; none reads a file the arm wrote about itself, none uses the arm's own tests or coverage.**

| Stage | What it covers (tagging rule) | (a) coverage by sealed probes, over criteria tagged to this stage and earlier | (b) executed behavioural checks | (c) scorer-side checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 skeleton / walking slice | the thinnest end-to-end path through every layer (one create, one read, the process starts, one persisted record) | 100% of S0 criteria | clean-clone build and start succeed; one real HTTP round trip | build and strict type check green |
| S1 core behaviour | the remaining happy-path behaviour of every feature | at least 1 - d1 of S0 and S1 (provisional d1 = 0.05) | the sealed S1 probe suite runs black box over HTTP | S0 checks hold |
| S2 edge cases and non-functional | validation and error paths, boundaries, pagination, idempotence, latency and size budgets the spec states | at least 1 - d2 of S0 to S2 (d2 = 0.10) | the NFR probes (latency, size) executed on a real data volume | strict type check green and no check that passed at S1 fails |

Removed from the first draft (both critics): the arm's own tests green, a coverage floor, "no unresolved gate waiver" and the open-questions column. Own-test status, coverage and ledger state are reported as **targeted**.

### 4.4 Chain end, the confirmation session and the cap

When the scorer first reports S4 passing, the harness runs **one confirmation session** with a fixed text identical in every arm ("review the repository against the specification and fix anything that does not meet it; if nothing, change nothing"), charged to the arm, then a final checkpoint. S4 is verified if it passes at both. **Cap:** 3 times B_4 in dollars for every arm, with the 60-session infrastructure limit; B_k = the **pooled median across all piloted arms** (C1, C2, C3, C4) of the cost to reach verified stage k in SDX-9-P `[FROM SDX-9-P]`, not the cheapest arm. Cost bands per stage are reported against 1 to 1.5x, 1.5 to 3x, beyond 3x of B_k. Clarification of P6 section 4: B_k comes from SDX-9-P, not from SDX-0, whose chains are ten-change extensions, not builds.

### 4.5 Margins (registered before data)

1. **Held-out post-complete suite and the escape bands.** The suite has two components authored by a person other than the probe author: **H_var**, held-out variants of acceptance criteria (the same behaviour exercised with different data and request shapes), and **H_unc**, intended behaviours the criteria do not cover (the spec's admitted incompleteness, share 1 - q of the intended behaviour, q declared by the independent reviewer). **Band on variants:** E_var, the share of H_var failing at the final snapshot, at or below **delta_v** is within tolerance; above it is **premature COMPLETE**. delta_v is fixed from **arm-free data only** (the reference implementation, the stage-labelled mutants and the stubs; provisional 0.05) and never from arm outcomes. **E_unc**, the share of H_unc failing, is reported descriptively against the reference value 1 - q with **no band**: agents pass many uncovered behaviours by sensible defaults, so a band on it would not detect overfitting to the visible criteria. (The first draft's single band (1 - q) + delta_e on the pooled suite is withdrawn for that reason.)
2. **Stage deviation d_k** (section 4.3).
3. **Budget bands** per stage (section 4.4); the single censoring rule (section 2.1).
4. **Open questions** are reported (asking rate per arm); there is no open-question margin and no gate.

## 6. Oracle and scorer (sealed, outside every repository, HTTP level)

The sealed oracle inherits SDX-1 section 6 (status-class tolerance with value and list-membership checks wherever the criterion defines them, paired probes scored as pairs, 2xx/4xx balance, fields checked only by the names the contract fixes), applied to the **final-state** probe set, extended so that every criterion has at least one probe with criterion id and stage tag; the mechanical SD rule is not used (nothing changes over time). **Stage scorer:** a script that clones the snapshot clean, builds, starts the process, runs the probes of the stage black box, runs the scorer-side checks and the SDX-9 open-field suite, extracts and runs the `quickstart` block, and prints the verified stage and per-criterion results; deterministic, versioned, hash-registered, run after every session; it sees no arm manifest and no arm label. Validation before any chain is read: reference at least 98% of probes, S4 and COMPLETE; stubs each at most 60% of probes and below S0; at least 90% of 20 hand mutants killed by the full suite; the 30 stage-labelled mutants of section 2b; oracle neutrality on planted ambiguities (the admissible sets are checked by the independent reviewer); a **leak check** that the held-out suite and the probes appear nowhere in the spec package or the arms' artifacts, and a **script-detected gaming check** (any arm repository containing request bodies identical to probe bodies). **Convention tolerance and gaming:** an always-4xx server fails paired probes; structure and naming are never checked. Untargeted secondary: duplication and cognitive complexity by a tool installed in no arm and run by no gate.

## 7. Judges

## 8. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Controls, manipulation checks, scorer validation and INVALID-DESIGN triggers (sections 2b and 9) before any contrast.
2. **H9-1 and H9-2 as one fixed-sequence family (no alpha spending, order fixed now).** Steps in order: (1) cost to verified COMPLETE, (2) S4, (3) S3, (4) S2, (5) S1, (6) S0. A step is **positive** if the 95% BCa interval of the log-cost difference classifies as a positive class (U < 0 and L < minus delta) against **each** comparator (C2 and C3; plus C5 if the Full scope is run; C2-B in place of C2 if supplied), non-completers entered at log cap. Stop at the first step that is not positive. **c\*** = the earliest step of the unbroken run of positive steps that ends at step (1). If step (1) is not positive, the registered statement is "no crossover within the cap" with the class at COMPLETE, and H9-1 is classified as in section 2.2. Fixed-sequence testing keeps the familywise level at 0.05 without correction at the price that an early crossover is credited only if every later step also passes ("crossed and stayed crossed"), which is intended. Strictness note: P6 section 7 words the crossover as "at or below ... with the interval excluding zero difference in the wrong direction"; this draft registers the stricter rule (a positive class), and reports as descriptive the **parity stage** p\* (earliest stage at which the interval lies inside minus delta to plus delta).
3. Sensitivities: actual-cost censoring; rank-based analysis with non-completers tied worst; survival analysis of cost to each stage with censoring at the cap (Kaplan-Meier curves, log-rank, Cox); the cache-neutral price table.
4. Secondary: H9-a and H9-b with Holm at 0.05; H9-c, H9-d, H9-e at 95% unadjusted and labelled; views V2 and V3; ITT and completers-only; the contrast-by-currency table (where currencies point to different rows the qualifier Q5 applies).
5. Exploratory list as in section 2.3. No further correction; nothing in the secondary list carries a verdict.

## 12. Threats to validity (declared)

**Targeted gates.** C4's gates execute the arm's own tests and the criteria-coverage ledger; the scorer uses only sealed probes and scorer-side checks the arm never sees; coverage, own-test status and the over-claim rate are reported as targeted with no verdict. **Spec in the substrate's native form:** numbered acceptance criteria are the exact input from which C4's ledger and coverage gate are generated, so "same information content" holds while the form favours the treatment;
