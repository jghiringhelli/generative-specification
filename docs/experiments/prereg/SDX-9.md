## 1. Question, and what it does and does not answer

Given a fixed, reviewed specification package with numbered acceptance criteria, how fast and at what cost does a coding agent under each regime reach each stage's machine-checked completeness criteria, up to a **verified COMPLETE** build, where "reached" is decided by a frozen scorer and never by the author or the agent; how often does the agent claim a stage before the scorer verifies it (false-complete); how many defects escape after COMPLETE is declared; and **at which stage, if any, does the substrate's cumulative cost, with setup and upkeep inside, fall below the comparators?**

It is the second of the two translations (spec to code and pipeline; the first, intent to spec, is outside) and asks how the machine-cheap translation behaves under each regime. It is **not** a head-start claim (the substrate has setup and upkeep cost, counted); not a claim about change over time (that is SDX-1 and SDX-8); not a claim that the spec was cheap to produce (the spec is an input; its production cost is outside and reported as not measured); not a claim about human developers (model-only first; the field version is the completeness measurement of the field study).

Honest prior, written before any data (protocol guard f; **DRAFT by the assistant, JC must confirm or overwrite before any data**): the substrate is slower or no faster than the expert prompt at S0 and S1 (H9-c, a registered risk: Gloaguen and others report more than 20% extra cost from context files without a success gain); a crossover is no earlier than S2, modal at S3 (where machine-checkable evidence of the real-runtime classes is cheap when gates exist and costly to discover otherwise); and "no crossover by COMPLETE within the cap" is a live outcome, probability near 35%, not a failure of the method's honesty.

Intuition in falsifiable form: "Building a fixed reviewed spec from an empty repository with a mid-tier coding agent, an enforced substrate reaches verified COMPLETE at least 15% cheaper than an expert prompt and than the same substrate with advisory gates, with escapes inside the spec's admitted incompleteness, and claims completion ahead of the scorer less often than an expert prompt." It is contradicted by H9-1 EQUIVALENT, REVERSED or INCONCLUSIVE, or by no crossover at any stage.

## 2. Hypotheses, readouts, falsifiers, controls

### 2.1 Readouts, defined once

Unit: one chain = one build from an empty repository plus the locked scaffold and the spec package, run by a stateless harness that opens fresh sessions (only the repository carries state, the SDX-1 rule) until the scorer reports S4 verified (plus one confirmation session, section 4.4) or the cap is hit. After every session the harness snapshots the working tree (a **checkpoint**) and the scorer runs on a clean clone.

- **Cost** (as in SDX-8 section 2.1): all model spend of every session and attempt, dollars from a price table registered at freeze, tokens second, agent wall-clock third. **View V1 (registered, verdict):** all in-chain spend including bootstrap and upkeep of the arm's own artifacts. **V2 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 minus the tokens the harness classifies, by file path, as writing or maintaining substrate files (setup and upkeep removed; expected favourable to C3 and C4). **V3 (labelled, no verdict):** V1 plus every arm's authoring hours at $75 per hour charged once per chain. Refutation row (ix) compares V1 with V2.
- **Stage reached and verified time.** A stage k is **verified complete at checkpoint j** when its four checks (section 4.3) pass at j and **at every later checkpoint through the end of the chain** (stickiness; a stage that later regresses is re-opened and the cost between the first pass and the final stable pass is charged to that stage). The cost to verified stage k is the cumulative cost through the first checkpoint from which stage k passes for good. Stages are cumulative: S(k+1) verified requires S(0..k) verified.
- **COMPLETE** = S4 verified and the escape rate E inside the band of section 4.5. A chain that verifies S4 but exceeds the upper band is a **premature COMPLETE** and counts as not complete.
- **Censoring.** A chain that does not reach a stage (or COMPLETE) by the cap is **censored at the cap cost**; it is analysed as censored (restricted mean, with survival sensitivity), never dropped.
- **Claim and verdict.** At the end of every session the agent must end its final message with a line `CLAIM: S0` to `CLAIM: S4` or `CLAIM: NONE`, per the same task prompt in every arm. **False-complete share** = (claims of stage k at a checkpoint whose verified stage is below k) / (all claims of a stage), per chain; regression events (re-opened stages) are counted separately.
- **Interventions:** scripted product-owner replies used, gate-failure loops, restarts (infrastructure only), harness nudges (one fixed text, in every arm, issued after three consecutive checkpoints with no new passing probe, at most three per chain; a defined event, not free text).
- **Verified throughput** VT = criteria-weighted verified criteria per dollar and per token at each stage and cumulatively (secondary).
- **Escapes** after S4: share of the held-out post-complete suite failing at the final snapshot (section 4.5), plus the secondary judge audit (section 7).

**Primary readout (one per trunk):** the cost to verified COMPLETE, per chain, on the log scale, restricted at the cap (H9-1). Everything else is secondary or targeted.

### 2.2 Classification of any contrast

Delta = mean of log X minus mean of log Y per chain (negative = X cheaper); classes CHEAPER-RELEVANT, CHEAPER-SMALL, CHEAPER-SIZE-UNRESOLVED, EQUIVALENT (TOST), COSTLIER (RELEVANT if p >= delta), INCONCLUSIVE, exactly as defined on the log scale in SDX-8 section 2.2. Primary family at 95% intervals (alpha 0.05 over the fixed sequence of section 8, no further correction); secondary hypotheses at 95% with Holm over H9-a and H9-b; BCa bootstrap (10,000 replicates) and Monte Carlo permutation (100,000), seeds registered; no stratification (one vendor, one model). SESOI delta = ln(0.85) in magnitude (15% in cost), provisional, with the justification to be written at the freeze (a smaller saving is probably not worth a heavy process whose human authoring and upkeep time sits outside model dollars). For rates (false-complete share, escape-in-band), SESOI 10 percentage points.

### 2b. Controls and validity gates (guard a)

- **Scorer sensitivity (positive control PC2).** 30 planted mutants of the reference implementation (6 per stage boundary), each with a known stage (the highest stage whose checks it still passes), plus the reference (must score S4 and, with its own tests, COMPLETE), degenerate stubs ("always 200", "always 4xx", empty repository: each must score below S0), and a stage-boundary suite built by deleting one criterion's behaviour at a time. The scorer must assign at least 95% of mutants the registered stage and never a higher one. Otherwise the scorer is repaired before any chain is read; if it cannot be: INVALID-DESIGN.
- **Dose check (positive control PC1).** C2 run at half the cap (n = 10): its median verified stage must be below that of C2 at the full cap, with the 95% interval excluding zero in the stage-reached score. Otherwise the cap and the scorer cannot register progress: INVALID-DESIGN.
- **Negative control C2b (A/A):** independent run of C2, n = 10. Noise trigger on cost to COMPLETE: the point difference between C2 and C2b at least delta with the 95% interval excluding zero means noise exceeds the effect: INVALID-DESIGN. Limit stated: noise is measured for C2 only; per-arm SDs are reported.
- **Negative control C4-stale (n = 10):** C4 whose criteria-ledger ids are wrong for a registered fraction (25% of entries, shuffled mechanically with a registered seed) and whose sentinel routing is wrong for the same fraction. It must not help: if C4-stale beats C2 in a CHEAPER class with a gap at least 0.8 of C4's, the gain follows presence and volume of files, not true ledger content: row (x); not INVALID-DESIGN.
- **Floor and ceiling.** C1 fails S0 in every chain: the spec package, scaffold or harness is defective: INVALID-DESIGN. C1 reaches verified COMPLETE in every chain with false-complete share below 0.05: H9-a and H9-b are not testable (ceiling): flagged, cost readouts stay interpretable. Censoring above 50% of chains in C2 or C4 at COMPLETE: the cap is too low for the primary: INVALID-DESIGN (a higher cap is a new registration). C4 fails S0 in more than 50% of its chains: C4 is at the floor, INVALID-DESIGN for its contrasts.
- **Planted-ambiguity channel check.** At least 80% of the planted ambiguities are asked about by at least one arm in the pilot, or the question channel is unusable: repair the channel before freeze.
- **Manipulation checks M1 and M2** inherited from SDX-1-ARMS section 4 for C1 and C2 (A5's text) and applied to C3, C4 and C5 (no element beyond what the arm defines; C5's hook is a generic gate only).

## 4. Fixture, spec package, stages, margins

### 4.1 The spec package and the fixture: Pastura-full

Pastura (invented, no public corpus, canary run cold per model, recall above zero is INVALID-DESIGN) with the locked scaffold of SDX-1 (Node, TypeScript strict, `node:http`, `node:sqlite`, vitest, fixed paths, hash-checked after every session), the spec package placed read-only in the repository (hash-checked like the scaffold), identical bytes in every arm. **Pastura-full** is defined as: the base spec and the **final-state resolution of SDX-1's ten changes** (the supersession of change 6 applied, the emergency move of change 3 retired by change 9, so the end-state probes are those live at SDX-1's snapshot 10) **plus an extension package E** written by independent authors, covering the criteria SDX-1 does not: validation and error paths, pagination, idempotence, latency and size budgets, the real-runtime classes of S3, and the run-from-clean-clone interface of S4. Size: at least 60 criteria (provisional; final size from SDX-9-P), each with at least one sealed executable probe carrying the criterion id and stage tag; at least 1.5x as many probes as criteria. Six planted ambiguities (two touching S1, two S2, one S3, one S4), each bound to one spec section, with scripted product-owner answers registered before data; the spec's declared completeness q (the share of intended behaviour the criteria claim to cover; definition in Compendium section 8.19, wording to be checked) is declared by the spec authors from an inventory of the reference implementation's behaviours audited by the independent reviewer.

**Stage definitions given to the agent** (in the task prompt): short plain-language versions of the first column of the table in section 4.3, with no thresholds. S4 requires a README containing a fenced block labelled `quickstart` whose commands the scorer extracts and runs in a clean container (the "stranger script").

### 4.2 The question channel (planted ambiguities)

Headless sessions cannot ask mid-session. The channel is the same in every arm: the agent may end a session with up to 5 lines `QUESTION[<spec section id>]: <text>` per session; the harness answers them in the next session's prompt (the scripted answer if the section carries a planted ambiguity, else the fixed sentence "no clarification beyond the text of that section"). Asking is logged, never penalized, and questions that cite a section without a planted ambiguity are counted as the spam rate (a metric, not a verdict). A planted ambiguity never asked about stays **open**. Deviation from P6 section 3, stated: P6's gate (d) accepts an ambiguity "asked and answered **or recorded**"; "recorded" is dropped because recording needs a persistent artifact that only C3 and C4 are instructed to write, and counting it would favour them. An ambiguity enters the coverage check (a) of its stage only after its scripted answer has been delivered, and its probe encodes the scripted resolution; before that it counts only in gate (d).

### 4.3 Stages and their machine-checkable completeness criteria

Each criterion is tagged to exactly one stage by the written rule of the second column, by the independent reviewer, before data. A stage is verified complete when all four checks hold (and stay holding, section 2.1). The thresholds d_k are **pre-declared allowed deviations**, provisional, to be justified at freeze from SDX-9-P (SESOI discipline of SDX-1 section 2.2).

| Stage | What it covers (tagging rule) | (a) coverage by sealed probes, over criteria tagged to this stage and earlier | (b) executed behavioural checks | (c) gates | (d) open-questions gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 skeleton / walking slice | the thinnest end-to-end path through every layer (one create, one read, the process starts, one persisted record) | 100% of S0 criteria | clean-clone build and start succeed; one real HTTP round trip | build and strict type check green | no planted ambiguity touching S0 is open |
| S1 core behaviour | the remaining happy-path behaviour of every feature | at least 1 - d1 of S0 and S1 (provisional d1 = 0.05) | the sealed S1 probe suite runs black box over HTTP | S0 gates plus the repository's own tests green | every ambiguity touching S1 asked and answered |
| S2 edge cases and non-functional | validation and error paths, boundaries, pagination, idempotence, latency and size budgets the spec states | at least 1 - d2 of S0 to S2 (d2 = 0.10) | the NFR probes (latency, size) executed on a real data volume | lint-class static checks green (type check, no new failures versus S1) | all S2 ambiguities closed |
| S3 hardening and verification | the real-runtime classes from an external defect taxonomy: persistence across restart, migration of the data file written at the S2 checkpoint by the arm's own build, concurrency, calendar and encoding boundaries | at least 1 - d3 of S0 to S3 (d3 = 0.10) | the sealed open-field suite (shared with SDX-5 Part B): real process, real data file, restart, network boundary, run by a deterministic script that sees no manifest | all gates green on a clean clone; line and branch coverage above a floor registered from SDX-0; an independent unit suite (SDX-5 D2b) passes | no open question |
| S4 release-ready | everything above, plus run-from-clean-clone documentation executed by the stranger script, no failing or skipped probe, no unresolved gate waiver | at least 1 - d4 of all criteria (d4 = 0.05) | the full sealed suite plus the clean-clone install script | all gates green on a clean clone | empty |

The "gates" columns are **scorer-side checks run by the harness identically for every arm**; they are not the arm's gates. A C4 gate and a scorer gate may run the same tool; the scorer's verdict never reads any file the arm wrote about itself.

### 4.4 Chain end and the confirmation session

When the scorer first reports S4 passing, the harness runs **one confirmation session** with a fixed text identical in every arm ("review the repository against the specification and fix anything that does not meet it; if nothing, change nothing"), charged to the arm, then a final checkpoint. The chain ends when S4 passes at that checkpoint (stickiness for S4), or the cap is hit. **Cap:** 40 sessions or 3 times B_4, whichever first, B_k = the SDX-9-P median cost of the cheapest arm to reach verified stage k `[FROM SDX-9-P]`. Cost bands per stage are reported against 1 to 1.5x, 1.5 to 3x, beyond 3x of B_k. Clarification of P6 section 4: B_k comes from SDX-9-P, not from SDX-0, whose chains are ten-change extensions, not builds.

### 4.5 Margins (registered before data)

1. **Completeness band from the spec itself.** The spec declares q before data. The **held-out post-complete suite** is composed of held-out variants of criteria (share q of the suite) and intended behaviours the criteria do not cover (share 1 - q), authored by a person other than the probe author. Escape rate E = share of the held-out suite failing at the final snapshot. Band: E at or below **(1 - q) + delta_e** (provisional delta_e = 0.05) is **within the spec's admitted incompleteness**; above is **premature COMPLETE**. Stated limit: an agent that gets every variant right and misses all uncovered behaviour has E = 1 - q, so the band is a ceiling on acceptable escapes, not a prediction (agents pass many uncovered behaviours by sensible defaults); SDX-9-P reports typical E per arm, and a band so loose that no arm exceeds it is flagged.
2. **Stage deviation d_k** (section 4.3).
3. **Budget bands** per stage (section 4.4); censoring at the cap.
4. **Open-question margin.** Zero open at S4; the registered per-stage numbers of section 4.3 earlier.

## 5. Model

One vendor, one mid-tier model through its headless CLI; dated snapshot id, CLI and Node versions recorded; all chains in a short window; arm order randomized in blocks of one chain per arm with C2b interleaved. A frontier cell (capacity moderator) and a second vendor are SDX-2-type replications, not pooled, only after a first result. Judges (spec vocabulary, ambiguity validity, escape audit, M1) are from another vendor than the generator; none is installed on this machine, so JC must supply API access before freezing.

## 6. Oracle and scorer (sealed, outside every repository, HTTP level)

The sealed oracle inherits SDX-1 section 6 (status-class tolerance with value and list-membership checks wherever the criterion defines them, paired probes scored as pairs, 2xx/4xx balance, fields checked only by the names the contract fixes), applied to the **final-state** probe set, extended so that every criterion has at least one probe with criterion id and stage tag; the mechanical SD rule is not used (nothing changes over time). **Stage scorer:** a script that clones the snapshot clean, builds, starts the process, runs the probes of the stage black box, runs the scorer-side gates and the open-field suite, extracts and runs the `quickstart` block, and prints the verified stage and per-criterion results; deterministic, versioned, hash-registered, run after every session; it sees no arm manifest and no arm label. Validation before any chain is read: reference at least 98% of probes and S4 and COMPLETE; stubs each at most 60% of probes and below S0; at least 90% of 20 hand mutants killed by the full suite; the 30 stage-labelled mutants of section 2b; oracle neutrality on planted ambiguities (the AMB probe encodes the scripted answer; the independent reviewer confirms that no other resolution is the only correct one in the spec text); a **leak check** that the held-out suite and the probes appear nowhere in the spec package or the arms' artifacts. **Convention tolerance and gaming:** an always-4xx server fails paired probes; structure and naming are never checked; targeted metrics (the criteria ledger, hook blocks, the arm's own test counts) are reported labelled "targeted" and carry no verdict. Untargeted secondary: duplication and cognitive complexity by a tool installed in no arm and run by no gate.

## 7. Judges

No primary readout is judged. Deterministic probes and recorded costs decide H9-1, H9-2, H9-a, H9-b. Judges (a different vendor, two vendors for any judged number, B11 validity first, human spot-check of a registered fraction) are used for: M1 leak and parity classification, the spec-vocabulary check, the ambiguity validity check, and the **secondary independent audit of every final repository** for escaped defects the held-out suite cannot see (no verdict). Deterministic scripts replace judges wherever they can decide.

## 8. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Controls, manipulation checks, scorer validation and INVALID-DESIGN triggers (sections 2b and 9) before any contrast.
2. **H9-1 and H9-2 as one fixed-sequence family (no alpha spending, order fixed now).** Steps in order: (1) cost to verified COMPLETE, (2) S4, (3) S3, (4) S2, (5) S1, (6) S0. A step passes if the 95% BCa interval of the log-cost difference has upper limit below zero against **each** comparator (C2 and C3; plus C5 if the Full scope is run), censored chains entered at the cap cost. Stop at the first failing step. **c\*** = the earliest step of the unbroken run of passing steps that ends at step (1). If step (1) fails, the registered statement is "no crossover within the cap" with the class at COMPLETE, and H9-1 is classified as in section 2.2. Fixed-sequence testing keeps the familywise level at 0.05 without correction; the price is that an early crossover is credited only if every later step also passes ("crossed and stayed crossed"), which is intended. Strictness note: P6 section 7 words the crossover as "at or below ... with the interval excluding zero difference in the wrong direction"; this draft registers the stricter rule (cheaper, interval excluding zero), and reports as descriptive the **parity stage** p\* (earliest stage at which the interval lies inside minus delta to plus delta, not costlier by delta).
3. Survival sensitivity: cost to each stage as a time-to-event on the dollar axis with censoring at the cap (Kaplan-Meier curves, log-rank, Cox); the restricted-mean analysis above is the registered one.
4. Secondary: H9-a and H9-b with Holm at 0.05; H9-c, H9-d and H9-e at 95% unadjusted and labelled; views V2 and V3; ITT and completers-only; the contrast-by-currency table.
5. Exploratory list as in section 2.3. No further correction; nothing in the secondary list carries a verdict.

## 12. Threats to validity (declared)

**Targeted gates.** C4's gates execute the arm's own tests and the criteria-coverage ledger; the scorer uses sealed probes the arm never sees; coverage by the ledger is reported as targeted with no verdict. **Spec quality and proponent authorship:** the spec package is written by us; the reviewer is independent; the same bytes for every arm; a stateless judge checks for GS vocabulary; the spec's criterion style may suit a ledger. **Spec given, not produced:** the trunk cannot show that producing the spec costs less than it saves, and says so. **Self-declared completeness:** the design exists to remove it; the scorer must be validated on mutants before any chain is read; the declared q and the band depend on an inventory made by the spec authors. **Stage design:** the tagging rule, the thresholds d_k and the cap are ours; a different tagging could move the crossover; the stage-wise cost depends on the checkpoint rhythm (after every session) and on the stickiness rule. **The question channel** may reward arms that ask more (cheap) or arms that ask less; asking is logged and spam counted but a strategy of asking about every section is legitimate behaviour and not penalized. **The confirmation session** is itself an intervention charged equally. **Authoring asymmetry:** C4 and C3 are GS-side artifacts, C2 and C5 an outsider's; the delta A4-S9 adds two gates the SDX-1 A4 did not have, which makes C4 here a different object from SDX-1's A4 (declared, a fork). **One fixture, one model, one oracle** (HYPOTHESES section 8); the fixture family is shared with SDX-1 and SDX-8 and **chains are not independent confirmations across trunks**; the open-field suite is shared with SDX-5 and SDX-8. **Cap and censoring:** a low cap hides a late crossover; the cap and horizon are registered; a higher cap is a new registration. **Model drift and vendor changes** (METR 2026 notes measurement problems of this kind): pin and log. **Interpretation of cost:** dollars depend on price tables and caching; wall-clock and tokens reported. **Intersection-union and fixed-sequence procedures are conservative;** low power for an early crossover is a property of the design. **Modal outcome near the SESOI** (section 2a). **Vendor-diverse critique has not happened;** the two Claude critics of SDX-8-9-REVIEW share training.
