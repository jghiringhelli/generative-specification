## 1. Question and what it is for

**How many changes does it take before spending tokens (and money) on remediating a legacy codebase repays itself, and does the GS substrate repay faster or slower than the same spend on ordinary tests and documentation?**

Primary estimand (revision 2): the **net cumulative difference D(k) = k (m_A - m_B) - R_B** at k = 60 and k = 200 accepted changes, in dollars at pinned prices, with an interval (linear in the estimates, no singularity); its sign change is the **break-even k\* = R_B / (m_A - m_B)**, reported with a Fieller interval as a translation of D(k), not as the quantity that assigns rows. The same for the cheap fix (D_C) and for B against C (D_BC). Also reported: the **GS overhead per change** (tokens spent reading and writing the substrate and running its gates), the **gross saving** on the work itself, and the net.

It is an **estimation study**. A result such as "it does not pay back within 200 changes" is a result, not a failure. It tests nothing about governance, defect escape in production, human productivity, or a client's willingness to pay; those are named in section 12.

Priors, stated as priors and not results: (1) JC and Gabriel: a one-time remediation pays for itself over time. (2) Direction from earlier work, all small n and not about remediation: a routed sentinel cut read cost by about 40% (TX), a map cut tokens per query from 234k to 78.6k (KX), and a chaotic twin of a repository cost 2,175k tokens against 351k for the lean twin (SX, n=2). Those are read-side savings. (3) Honest rival: frontier models navigate unfamiliar code natively (SX pilot: they found all copies without a search tool, at about four times the cost), so the per-change saving could be small and the break-even long. (4) A second rival: a cheap fix (tests and a README, no substrate) gets most of the saving at lower cost, and that is what the field developer did.

## 2. Arms

Every arm starts from the same cloned legacy codebase ("point 1"), the same model, the same tools (read, edit, shell, git, test runner; web and MCP disabled), the same per-session caps and the same hidden acceptance. Arms differ in what is done to the repository before the change items, and in the standard wrapper text of the change sessions.

## 3. Fixtures: the legacy codebases (the units of replication)

**Count and size.** Four codebases of 8 to 25 kLOC each (at least 3 are required; the fourth buys the codebase-level estimate, see section 9). Two strata:

- **R (real, third party).** Open source projects with a permissive licence (MIT, BSD or Apache-2), chosen by **an independent person** (not JC, not the GS side, no exposure to the formulas) by a written rule frozen before looking: at least 5 years since the first commit, at least 300 commits and at least 5 authors, a runnable black-box surface (command line or HTTP) with at least 25 distinguishable behaviours, and no test suite or one covering at most 10% of lines, no documentation beyond a short README, no instruction file. Selected, never edited; if a project has tests or docs, it is not chosen (removing them would be an author act). Credibility: real mess, real history, real surprises; risks: **memorization** (the model may know the project and its upstream fixes) and **uncontrolled provenance**. Controls: the items are authored new, never taken from the upstream tracker; a cold canary per model asks for the project's upstream conventions and a known upstream fix; the canary is also run on a fabricated control project of matched style and size; recall exceeding the control by at least 2 of 5 probes marks the pair as contaminated and it is reported apart; candidate R projects are screened by the canary before selection, not after.
- **S (synthetic, grown).** A codebase grown by an **ungoverned model session** (a fixed vendor **not among the experiment vendors**, or one S codebase per experiment vendor with the generator-vendor cells reported apart; if the maturity script fails, regrow with the next registered seed, at most three seeds, every attempt reported; if all three fail, "model-grown code does not reach maturity" is the finding for that stratum; no spec, no tests, no architecture guidance, no refactoring request) through 40 to 60 feature requests written in sequence by an independent person who describes a product in business terms, until it reaches 15 to 25 kLOC. Credibility: not memorized, maturity and size controllable, no licence question; risks: **model-shaped mess may be easier for a model to navigate than human-written legacy**, so a positive result on S alone would be discounted, and a null on S alone would be too.

**Which is more credible.** Neither alone. R has ecological validity and the memorization risk; S is clean and has the "models read their own mess" risk. The default is **two of each**, reported by stratum, with the pooled estimate secondary. If the strata disagree on the sign or on the order of magnitude of k\*, that is reported as the finding and no pooled claim is made. A third option, human-written code under time pressure by a paid freelancer, is the upgrade if a reader doubts both (cost not estimated here).

**Maturity (operational, checked by a frozen script before the items are authored; the script and thresholds are hashed).** A codebase is "mature spaghetti" when all hold: (1) changed many times: R at least 300 commits, S at least 40 feature rounds; (2) 8 to 25 kLOC and at least 3 modules; (3) at least 25 black-box behaviours that a person can state; (4) tests absent or at most 10% line coverage; (5) documentation at most a one-page README and no instruction file; (6) structural smell: at least three of {longest file, 90th-percentile function complexity, dependency cycles, duplicated-line share, mutable global count} above the 75th percentile of a reference set of ten projects drawn at random (seeded) from the same language, 8 to 40 kLOC, at least 300 commits and coverage of at least 50%; (7) two persons other than the GS side, independently, read a sample of files and attest that the code is hard to change. The GS checker may be run and reported (expected: ABSENT on at least 10 of 12 items) but is **descriptive only**, because it is the GS side's own instrument.

## 4. Point 2: the batch of changes (the unit of cost)

Per codebase, **60 items**: 40 change requests (feature, modification) and 20 bug fixes, written as user-facing tickets in plain language by persons who meet the independence definition of section 3 (not JC, not the GS side, no exposure to the formulas); any model assistance in authoring items, tests or suites uses a vendor not among the experiment vendors and is logged per item; **at least one codebase's items are written, and its traps chosen, by a person who doubts GS**, the skeptic decides any ambiguity veto raised by the GS side; vetoed items are run anyway and reported as a sensitivity. Bugs: at least two thirds found by an independent tester or by differential execution (run original and a candidate, compare), at most one third planted and labelled.

**Hidden acceptance.** Per item, black-box tests at the product's surface (command line or HTTP), authored by the tester from the original running behaviour and the ticket, sealed and hashed before any arm runs, never visible to any arm; surface-level so that arm D's different stack can be tested by the same files. An item **counts as accepted only if it passes its hidden tests and the sealed characterization suite** (below). **The behaviours targeted by the 20 bug items are excluded from the characterization suite**; a bug item found already fixed after remediation is scored *pre-solved* (its change cost is zero), results are reported with and without pre-solved items, and an arm pre-solving more than 3 items is itself reported as a finding. Quality is therefore held constant by the definition of the outcome, not by assumption; acceptance rate per arm is reported next to cost.

**Regression.** A sealed characterization suite of 60 to 120 behaviours per codebase, captured from the original and authored independently, run after every item. At least **15 of the 60 items are traps**: the natural implementation breaks a behaviour that is implicit in the code and not stated anywhere. Traps are classified before freezing by two raters (the tester and the skeptic) using only the original code and a black-box run, as *discoverable* (a characterization of the code could find the rule) or *latent*; kappa is reported and disagreements count as latent; results are reported by class so that the remediation is not blamed or credited for latent ones.

**Independence of items.** Every item runs as a **fresh session on its own branch from the point-1 snapshot of its arm** (after remediation for B, C, B', D), never chained. This gives many independent cost observations and no carry-over, and it **does not measure erosion that accumulates over a chain** (SDX-8-9-REVIEW: single-chain designs were underpowered). Declared consequence, in both directions: the continue arm's cost does not grow in the primary analysis, **and the remediation arms' substrate is never stale** (docs and tests are in sync with the code at every item), so both are flattered; symmetric growth parameters are in section 10. A **chain probe** (one codebase per vendor, 12 items applied cumulatively to a "point 2" state, hidden suite at the end, per arm) supplies the growth slopes g_A, g_B, g_C of section 10 (the estimates are used there, not only shown), is otherwise descriptive, tier D, and answers "how much does it cost to leave the codebase functional at point 2" as a single path.

**Attempts.** One attempt per item with a session cap `[FROM REM-0]`; if the hidden tests fail, one second attempt receives only the names of the failed acceptance behaviours (never the tests; for failures of the characterization suite on trap items only "a regression was detected"). All spend counts toward the item. **First-attempt acceptance and cost are co-primary with the two-attempt figures; if they assign different rows, both are reported and neither is claimed.** Items not accepted after two attempts count as unaccepted in the acceptance denominator, with their spend in the cost numerator and the registered fallback cost of section 6.

## 5. Models and vendors

At least **three vendors**, modern frontier tier at freeze, versions pinned by dated id and logged `[AT FREEZE]`, at least two not Anthropic, each in its own coding-agent CLI or an API harness of ours. **Metering is a hard requirement.** The Copilot-PC pattern (one fresh chat per model, `COPILOT-CRITIC-RUNBOOK.md`) is adequate for critics and judges but **does not expose token counts**, so it cannot carry the cost outcome. Cost cells need vendor API keys with usage logs, used through our one harness (section 2e); a vendor that cannot be metered is run only as a **descriptive, requests-counted cell** and excluded from the cost estimate. Securing three metered vendors before freezing is precondition 3 of section 14. Each vendor is a stratum, not a replicate of the same population: vendor-specific results are shown (descriptive), and the pooled estimate is defined once, in sections 6 and 10.

## 6. Outcomes and the cost ledger

**Primary (cost).** For each arm and cell, **m = (spend per attempted item) / (acceptance rate)**, both factors reported: spend counts all tokens of the change sessions, failed and second attempts included, and the substrate work inside those sessions (classifier categories (b) and (c) below; there is no separate "upkeep" outside the sessions because sessions are fresh). Every item unaccepted after two attempts also carries a fallback cost `[AT FREEZE]` (default: twice the session cap in dollars plus the human-proxy cost of a manual fix), so that the cumulative curve counts every ticket delivered. For B, C and D the **remediation cost R** = all tokens of the remediation sessions (retries and abandoned runs included) per replicate, **realized** (C-natural's R_C may be below R_B). Cumulative cost after k accepted changes: `C_A(k) = k m_A`, `C_B(k) = R_B + k m_B`, `C_C(k) = R_C + k m_C`. **D(k) = C_A(k) - C_B(k)**, and **D_BC(k) = (R_C - R_B) + k (m_C - m_B)** with realized R_C. Break-even k* = R_B / (m_A - m_B) (infinite if the denominator is not positive) is reported with a Fieller interval, descriptively. Pooled estimate: equal-weight mean over cells of R and of (m_A - m_B), in dollars at pinned list prices; D, D_BC and k* are computed from the pooled means. **Rows of section 11 are assigned on the dollar metric only**; token results are reported per vendor and per category and are descriptive.

**Ledger.** Tokens in four categories (uncached input, cache write, cache read, output), reported per category and per vendor and never summed; **dollars** at the vendors' list prices pinned at freeze (a second price scenario with cache reads at 10% and one with all prices at plus and minus 50% as sensitivity). Wall-clock minutes and turn counts per session.

**GS overhead, measured explicitly (arm B and B' only).** From each transcript a deterministic classifier assigns the tokens of every turn to one of: (a) *reading substrate* (map, spec sections, decision records, ledger), (b) *writing substrate* (spec edits, ids, decision records, ratchet line, commit message rules), (c) *gates* (running `gs-check`, `gs-lock`, hooks, fixing a gate failure, false blocks), (d) *work* (reading and editing production code, running its tests). Classifier rules are frozen and hashed before data; its validity is checked on 150 turns stratified by arm and class, labelled by two persons who are not from the GS side and are blind to arm (token-weighted Cohen's kappa, lower 95% bound at least 0.75). If it fails, only the overhead decomposition is unevaluable; the primary D(k) does not use the classifier. **Overhead per change = (a)+(b)+(c); gross saving = m_A minus the (d) cost of B; net = m_A - m_B.** The same classifier runs on A and C with the same path classes, so that tests and notes written by the agent in A and C are classified symmetrically (what the arm writes for itself is reported, not charged to a "substrate").

**Quality guards (co-reported, not folded into cost).** Acceptance rate; regression rate on the sealed characterization suite; trap-item pass rate by class; the share of sessions that hit the cap; false blocks by gates; abandoned remediation runs.

**Human-time proxy (sensitivity only; it never assigns a row).** Not a measured human time. Per change: lines of diff a reviewer must read (production-code lines counted apart from test, doc and substrate lines), and the number of decisions put to a human (ratifications requested, questions asked); the scripted reply texts are hashed at freeze, and replies approve or reject by a registered rule. Minutes per decision and per 100 diff lines `[AT FREEZE]`, hourly rate `[AT FREEZE]`. A timed sample (at least 4 remediation runs reviewed by a hired engineer, minutes recorded) calibrates the minutes and is mandatory if the proxy is reported; it costs paid human time (section 9).

## 7. Blinding and judges

Item authors and testers never see arm outputs. The acceptance is mechanical. Judged material is limited to: (1) whether a diff implements the ticket in a way the hidden tests cannot see (a sample of 10% of accepted items per arm, to detect gaming of the tests, e.g. special-casing), and (2) the maintainability reading of remediation outputs. Maintainability readings of remediation outputs are declared **unblinded** (substrate files identify the arm); diff-level judging strips substrate paths first. Judges are **from a vendor other than the one that produced the output**, blind to arm, calibrated on a planted-flaw set first (judge-validity check); if no non-generator judge is available, the judged outcomes are declared unevaluable, not substituted. JC does not read blind pairs here (he is the stakeholder); the skeptic reads a sample of 30 diffs with substrate paths stripped, reported as the only human reading.

## 8. Ceiling, floor, controls, validity triggers

- **Headroom.** Checked per codebase on 10 calibration items never used in the study, before freezing. Arm A acceptance above 85% does not invalidate the cost outcome (a world where models navigate native code is a legitimate answer); acceptance-based and trap rows are declared unevaluable for that codebase. Below 30%, items are replaced only from a reserve pool (at least 15 per codebase, authored and hashed before freezing) drawn at random by the independent author, never chosen by inspecting which items A failed. Any arm above 95% acceptance in a cell is reported as a ceiling for that contrast. Cap-hit share above 15% in any arm: raise the cap by the rule `[AT FREEZE]` and rerun the cell.
- **Contamination.** Canary per model and codebase (section 3).
- **Emergent substrate detector.** A deterministic scan of every A, C and C' final repository reports ids, ledgers, instruction files, hooks and ratchets that appeared. If C built a substrate, it is reported as a mediator and the arm is not renamed.
- **Remediation is analysed by intention to treat.** The first remediation's final state enters the item phase whatever the sealed suite says; the rerun (one, for B and C) is a sensitivity analysis only. The post-remediation regression rate on the characterization suite (excluding the bug-targeted behaviours) is a quality-guard outcome, not a validity trigger. A remediation that changes behaviour outside a logged bug log is a substantive negative result about remediation, reported as such. The overlap between behaviours written down by B or C and the sealed suite is computed and acceptance is also reported on the non-overlapping behaviours (the suite is the same construct as C's characterization tests).
- **Positive control.** At least 8 items per codebase whose rule is stated in code comments or visible behaviour at point 1 (*discoverable*, authored independently): each of B and C must exceed A's pass rate by at least 20 points with the 97.5% interval excluding zero; otherwise that arm's rows carry the label "premise failed".
- **Harness persistence test** (planted memory, as in SDX-1 section 3); unique repository path; memory off. A hook that blocks all sessions is a harness fault, not a result.
- **Pilot REM-0** (section 9): excluded data.

## 9. Sample size, power and cost (assumptions, not results)

Plan: 4 codebases x 3 vendors = 12 cells; 60 items per cell and arm; 2 remediation replicates per cell for B and C (24 B runs, 24 C runs); items in B and C assigned at random across the two replicates of their cell. Sessions: A, B, C and B' 720 each, C' 360: about 3,240 first attempts, about 4,100 with second attempts. D (2 codebases x 3 vendors, 30 items): about 180 more sessions plus 6 remediation runs.

**The independent units are fewer than the sessions.** Items are many (720 per arm) but the codebases are four: inference is conditional on these four codebases and does not extend to a population of legacy code; four is the minimum for a rough statement and per-codebase estimates are shown beside the pooled one.

**Simulation** (`simulate.py`; every effect, noise and cost value is an assumption; 120 simulated experiments per cell; 95% bootstrap over items and remediation replicates; R in units of one accepted continue-arm change; s = per-change saving of B over A). Classes: PAYS_BACK (upper bound of k\* at most 60), NOT_WITHIN_60 (lower bound above 60), NEVER_PRACTICAL (lower bound above 200), INCONCLUSIVE.

- The design is **decisive (inconclusive probability 0.15 or less) in most of the grid**, because the interval of k\* is narrow when the saving is large and the verdict "not within 60 or 200" is easy when R is large. At R of 30 changes: s = 0.15 gives NOT_WITHIN_60 at 0.84 (true k\* 200); s = 0.30 gives 0.74 with 0.25 inconclusive (true k\* 100); s = 0.50 (true k\* 60) is the hard point: 0.22 pays back, 0.18 not within 60, 0.60 inconclusive.
- **It is inconclusive when the true k\* sits within a factor of about 1.5 of the horizon** (R = 10, s = 0.15: k\* about 67, inconclusive 0.79). The typical 95% interval for k\* is about plus and minus 35% when s is 0.3 or more (R = 30, s = 0.3: median 69 to 134) and spans a factor of 2.5 to 4 when s is 0.15 (median 113 to 491). The honest answer in that region is the interval, not a class.
- **Small savings:** with s of 0.05 or less and R of 30 or more the design says "does not pay back within 200 changes" with probability 0.39 to 1.0 and is rarely inconclusive (R = 30, s = 0.05: 0.60 not within 60, 0.39 never practical, 0.01 inconclusive). With R = 10 and s of 0 to 0.05 it is inconclusive 0.31 to 0.41. **This is the "does not pay back" result and it counts.**
- Sensitivity at R = 30: 30 items instead of 60 raises inconclusive at s = 0.30 from 0.25 to 0.36 and 100 items lowers it to 0.10; a third remediation replicate lowers it only slightly; three codebases give 0.32 against 0.25; noisier items (item SD 1.2, session SD 0.8) raise it to 0.40; if B passes 10 points more items than A, the saving per accepted change grows and PAYS_BACK at s = 0.30 rises to 0.06 (but NOT_WITHIN_60 falls to 0.50 and inconclusive 0.44).
- **Known defect, a precondition (14.9):** the simulated bootstrap covered the conditional truth only 0.68 to 0.94 of the time (liberal at the hard points; with two replicates a bootstrap over replicates has three distinct resamples and understates their variance). Widening 95% to 97.5% does not repair that. The registered procedure (section 10) is a two-stage cluster bootstrap, and **`simulate.py` must be rerun with exactly that procedure, a replicate effect on m_B, 97.5% intervals and the REM-0 variances, and must reach coverage of at least 0.93 at every grid point, or the replicates are raised to 4 per cell, before freezing.** The class probabilities above were computed with the liberal procedure and are optimistic.
- **Not simulated yet (owed, precondition 14.9):** the B-versus-C contrast and its classes (D_BC with margins), acceptance differences in both directions, symmetric growth, and the code path of the primary D(k). The decision table is not powered until that is done.

**Cost (model only, guesses until REM-0 measures them).** Change sessions about 4,100 at 1.5 to 5 dollars: `$6k to $20k`. Remediation: 48 runs at 60 to 400 dollars (10 to 25 kLOC): `$2.9k to $19k`; D adds `$1.8k to $9k`. Judges and extra reviews 10 to 15%. Core (A, B, C, B', C') about `$9k to $39k`, central about `$21k`; with C-matched and D up to `$50k`. A reduced REM-1-Lite (3 codebases, 40 items, 2 replicates, no C') is about `$4.5k to $22k`. Preparation is the larger cost and is paid in agent-assisted hours and independent persons: 240 items with hidden tests, four characterization suites, two curated real codebases and two grown ones, trap classification, an independent tester: a guess of `[150 to 300]` agent-assisted hours plus paid independent persons.

**Alternative allocations the second critic proposed (for JC; listed in the private report):** 8 codebases x 30 items x 2 vendors (a statement about a population of codebases, but fewer than three vendors); 3 cumulative chains of 40 items per arm per codebase (measures growth directly); a one-vendor factorial settling wrapper and budget. The simulation says items beyond 60 barely move the inconclusive rate while codebases do (3 codebases 0.32, 4 codebases 0.25, 6 codebases 0.16 at s = 0.30, R = 30).

**Staging.** REM-0 (2 codebases, one real and one synthetic, 1 vendor, 20 items, **2 replicates** of B and C, about `$1k to $3k`) measures R, m, the SDs including the between-replicate and between-codebase components as far as two units allow (components it cannot estimate are varied over a registered range in the simulation), acceptance in A and classifier agreement; its data are excluded. **Go/no-go uses only the variance estimates, never the pilot's point estimate of the saving** (that would condition the study on its own effect): run the full study if the simulated inconclusive probability over the registered prior range of s is at most 0.30 on both axes of section 11, or if JC accepts the interval as the product (JC's rule: run nothing whose probable result is inconclusive).

## 10. Analysis plan, exactly as registered

1. Per cell: `m_A`, `m_B`, `m_B'`, `m_C`, `m_C'` (spend per attempted item divided by acceptance), `R_B`, `R_C` per replicate, as defined in section 6. Pooling: equal-weight mean over cells (vendors and codebases) of R and of the savings, in dollars at pinned prices; D(k), D_BC(k) and k* computed from the pooled means.
2. Interval: 97.5%, **two-stage cluster bootstrap**: resample replicates within cell, then items within the resampled replicate (stratified by item type and trap class), with the n/(n-1) variance inflation; for pooled rows also resample codebases (if that interval is too wide to assign a row, only per-codebase rows are assigned and the pooled row is "unassigned"). D(k) at k = 60 and 200 takes its interval from the bootstrap draws; k* by Fieller, descriptive. Per codebase and per vendor shown, descriptive.
3. Contrasts: D_B (B vs A), D_C (C-natural vs A), **D_BC** (primary for the B-versus-C question), D_B' and D_C' (wrapper checks), at k = 60 and 200.
4. **Extrapolation beyond 60 accepted changes** is linear with the observed means plus the growth sensitivity: cost per change grows by g_A, g_B, g_C per change (grid {0, 0.5%, 1%} each, including g_B > g_A), with slopes estimated from the chain probe's token series (categories (a) and (b) against item index for g_B); the primary uses g = 0 and is labelled as flattering both A and the remediation arms.
5. Sensitivities (descriptive; none can change a row): tokens by category; two price scenarios; with and without the human-time proxy; first attempts only; only items accepted in all arms (paired subset); excluding contaminated pairs; excluding latent traps; excluding pre-solved items; status-stopped versus cap-stopped B runs; vetoed items.
6. No hypothesis test is registered. Classes are assigned from the intervals above.

## 11. Decision table (registered; rows on the dollar metric; Axis 0 first; then three axes read independently and reported as a cross-product, not top to bottom)

**Axis 0, INVALID-DESIGN.** Arm A at a floor (below 30%) in more than half of the cells after replacement, contamination in all pairs of a stratum, the harness persistence test failing, or the positive control failing in both B and C. (Fewer than two metered vendors is not a result: the study is not frozen, see 14.3.) Report the failure; no cost statement; fix and rerun.

**Axis 1, B against A (payback), from D_B(k).** Let [l60, u60] and [l200, u200] be the 97.5% intervals of D_B(60) and D_B(200).

## 12. Threats to validity (declared)

1. **Four codebases** (section 9): conditional inference, no population statement; heterogeneity across codebases may dominate.
2. **Tokens are not the client's cost.** A client's real cost is mostly human time and consultant fees; the study measures tokens and a human-time proxy. It cannot say when a 20,000-dollar engagement repays itself (section 6 of the private report gives the model that sales fills with the client's numbers).
3. **Independent items from point 1** omit accumulated erosion and interaction among changes. The continue arm is flattered (its cost does not grow) and so are the remediation arms (their substrate is never stale): the direction of the net bias is unknown, which is why growth parameters are symmetric in section 10.
4. **Wrapper confounded with substrate** (B vs A uses formula 5 text); B' separates it on two codebases only.
5. **The cheap-fix steelman depends on one practitioner's prompt**; a different practitioner could do better or worse. Field data from the real case (companion request) can inform it but must not be used as the arm unless the practitioner agrees.
6. **Authoring bias** in items, traps and bugs; mitigated by independent authors and a skeptic stratum, not eliminated. Traps' classification (discoverable vs latent) is a judgement.
7. **Models and prices move**; the study is dated to the pinned versions and prices; a repeat in six months may read differently. Prompt caching changes the token mix between vendors.
8. **Remediation output varies run to run** (2 replicates per cell is a small sample of that variability).
9. **Memorized real code** (section 3) and **model-shaped synthetic code**.
10. **Critics are of one family** (section 15).
11. **GS-side instrument:** `gs-check` decides when B's remediation stops; if it is wrong, B stops early or late. The registered cap bounds this and the stop status is reported.
12. **No human practitioners, no novice GS user:** the learning cost of the substrate for a human team is not in these numbers.

## 13. What the result licenses for the offer (committed here; hashed at freeze)

- Axis 1 NOT WITHIN 200 or REVERSED, or Axis 2 C AHEAD: the claim "remediation pays for itself" is withdrawn from all sales material; the Remediation offer is not priced or argued on token savings; the case, if kept, rests on governance and risk that this study did not measure and is labelled so.
- Axis 2 EQUIVALENT: the cheap tier (tests and docs) is offered first and GS remediation is sold on grounds other than cost.
- Axis 1 PAYS or LATE with Axis 2 GS AHEAD: the sentence "in [N] codebases with these models and prices, remediation repaid its token cost within [interval] accepted changes and was cheaper per change than the same spend on tests and docs" may be used, with the stratum, vendors, dates and the human-proxy caveat.
- Any INCONCLUSIVE: no cost sentence; the interval is published as is.
- Always, regardless of the result: no general "pays back in N changes", no dollars for a particular client without that client's numbers (the break-even model in the private report is a model), no claim about governance value, human productivity or defects in production, and the field case is not evidence.
