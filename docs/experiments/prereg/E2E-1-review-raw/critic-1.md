# Critic 1: statistical validity and power, E2E-1 draft rev 1

Recomputation first. Section 9 arithmetic is correct: with SD 12, two-sided 97.5% (z 2.24) and 80% power (z 0.84), the minimum detectable difference is 8.7 for 36 vs 36, 12.3 for 18 vs 18, 17.4 for the interaction and 6.2 at 144 runs. The problems are in what the arithmetic leaves out.

1. BLOCKER, section 8 vs section 10. The ceiling trigger (weaker arm at or above 95%) is inconsistent with the SESOI of 8 on P-ACC. If the weaker arm sits at 92% or higher, no gain of 8 points exists, so POSITIVE is impossible while the design is not flagged. Fix: set the headroom trigger from the SESOI, for example weaker arm at or below 100 minus 2 x SESOI (84 for P-ACC, 80 for P-REG), and apply it to the main-run data too, not only to the pilot.

2. BLOCKER, sections 9, 10, 11 (rows c, i, j). EQUIVALENT is nearly unreachable. With SD 12 and n 36 vs 36 the half-width is 6.3 against a SESOI of 8, so the point estimate must lie within 1.7 points (about 45% chance even if the true effect is 0). At SD 15 the half-width is 7.9 and the chance is about 2%. For simple effects (18 vs 18) the half-width is 9.0 at SD 12, larger than the SESOI, so EQUIVALENT cannot occur. Per vendor (12 vs 12) it is 11. Rows c, i and the vendor clause "no vendor is equivalent" are therefore mostly unreachable, and a null will almost always print as INCONCLUSIVE. Fix: state the probability of each row under the planning truths (0, SESOI/2, SESOI, 2 x SESOI), choose the equivalence interval level (90% TOST is conventional), or widen the SESOI; delete row i and the vendor-equivalence clause if they cannot be reached.

3. BLOCKER, section 11. The table has gaps and overlaps. Gaps: P-ACC positive with P-REG equivalent (mirror of b); either positive with the other inconclusive; one positive with one reversed; one equivalent with one reversed; an interval excluding zero but inside (0, SESOI), which the classes do not name ("positive-small" in row e is undefined); intervals that span minus SESOI. Overlaps: a, g, h, i, j and f can all hold at once, with no precedence rule. Row k (all arms at ceiling) is an observed outcome that collides with row c and is not among the section 8 triggers. Row d's cell ("p <= minus SESOI is REVERSED-RELEVANT") is garbled. Row e fires only when co-primaries are "equivalent or positive-small", so a large gain with 50% higher cost has no row. Fix: define a partition on the pair (class of P-ACC, class of P-REG), 4 x 4 cells with a conclusion each, then treat the interaction, simple effects, vendor and cost rows as separate labelled families.

4. MAJOR, section 9. P-REG has no power calculation. It is a share of 8 to 10 trap steps per run, so binomial noise alone is about 15 points SD at p 0.7 (about 10 at p 0.9), and the denominator is conditioned on a post-treatment state: a run with fewer passing carried probes has less to regress. SD 12 cannot be assumed for it, and SESOI 10 may then be unreachable. Fix: compute P-REG power separately; define regression per probe that passed before the step (probe-level denominator); analyse probe-level counts with a run random effect or a beta-binomial model.

5. MAJOR, sections 3, 9, 12. The independent units for the claims are fewer than 72. Each project has one substrate build, one specification, one trap list and one practitioner prompt; the three repetitions share all of them, and only two projects exist. Repetitions estimate session noise, not variation between substrates, prompts or projects. Effect heterogeneity across projects cannot be estimated with two. Fix: word every claim as conditional on this substrate instance, this prompt author and these two projects; report the effect per project; if the claim is to be about "the bundle", build at least two independent substrate instances per project or drop the claim.

6. MAJOR, section 8, 14.6. E2E-0 (one project, one vendor, one run per arm) yields no within-cell variance, so it cannot supply the SD or support the headroom decision (n=1 against an SD of 12). The negative control X-b (6 runs) has power near 10% against a SESOI-sized difference, so "no flag" is not evidence of low noise; the positive control (5 runs, threshold 90%) has a 5-run binomial interval of roughly 55% to 100%. X-b, the controls and the pilot are not in the 72 runs or the cost line. Fix: run at least 3 repetitions of X in the pilot, take the SD from pooled X and X-b, add the control runs to the cost, and state whether X-b is pooled into X.

7. MAJOR, section 10 item 4 and row j. "Sign agrees in all three vendors" is a noisy rule: with a true null the chance of three equal signs is 25%, and per-vendor SE is about 4.9 points at SD 12, so sign flips are expected for true effects up to about 8. "Mixed" will be declared mostly by chance. Fix: replace by a heterogeneity test or interval on the vendor-by-substrate interaction, with its power stated, and report per-vendor intervals. Vendor and project cannot be random effects with 3 and 2 levels; say fixed and restrict inference to them.

8. MAJOR, sections 6, 10. A linear model on a bounded share makes the interaction scale-dependent: near the ceiling the substrate gain shrinks mechanically for the better prompt, producing rows g or h without any mechanism. Fix: register the scale (logit of probe-level pass), report the interaction on it, or label the interaction as raw-scale only.

9. MAJOR, section 10 item 2. "Cluster-robust or permutation" is undecided; cluster-robust errors are invalid with two projects and one cluster per run. Fix: register one method, for example a permutation test that shuffles substrate labels within prompt x vendor x project strata, with the seed and the number of permutations.

10. MAJOR, sections 6, 10, 11. Multiplicity is incoherent. Alpha 0.025 each suits the union claim of row b but is conservative for row a (both positive), while rows g to j, the cost rows and per-vendor claims carry "permitted conclusions" though labelled "no verdict", with no alpha. Cost row e uses a 20% threshold with an unstated interval level, no variance assumption, and no power (cost per accepted change is a heavy-tailed ratio; with CV 40% and n 36 vs 36 the half-width is about 21%, equal to the threshold). The amortization length and the human hour rate are unspecified. Fix: name the confirmatory family (two co-primaries plus the cost contrast) and its alpha; mark all else descriptive without "permitted conclusions"; give the cost model, interval level and rate.

11. MINOR, section 9. SD 12 is an assumption borrowed from single-shot runs; build-then-change runs compound failures and may be wider. Use t, not z, quantiles (about 2.30 at 65 df), and add a sensitivity table at SD 8, 12, 15 and 20.

12. MINOR, section 9. "Per-vendor cells (6 runs)" is the arm-by-vendor cell; the per-vendor substrate contrast has 12 vs 12. Restate which cell is meant.

13. MINOR, section 11 closing claims. "Inconclusive for a gap of about 5 points or less" understates it: at SD 12 a true gap equal to the SESOI of 8 is detected only about 75% of the time on the main effect, and about 35% on a simple effect. State power at the SESOI itself.
