# Design brief: paper M (evaluation methods and negative results), first paper to design (2026-10-05)

Status: design brief, not a draft; nothing submitted. Extends `M-skeleton.md` (2026-10-03). Companion: `C:\workspace\PragmaWorks\soma\docs\reports\2026-10\paper-tree-assessment-2026-10-05.md` section 4 (schedule) and `TREE-REVISION-2026-10-05.md`.

**Thesis.** A method whose own text can define the metric invites evaluation errors that look like results; one programme's complete record of its own evaluations shows which errors occurred, and a protocol plus a short checklist would have caught them.

**Contribution (four, none a claim that GS works).** (1) A case series of every logged experiment, each classified by validity defect, selection rule "all entries". (2) A defect catalogue for treatments that define their own metric, with the targeted-versus-untargeted rule and INVALID-DESIGN as a registered outcome. (3) A checklist for authors, reviewers and replicators. (4) A prospective reliability check: a pre-registered blind classification of the entries by readers of other vendors, with agreement reported.

**Outline (10 pages plus 2 of references, the RENE format).** 1 Introduction (0.75 p). 2 Background: LLM-study guidelines, same-family judge bias, benchmark inflation, construct validity, registered reports (0.75). 3 Case-series method: all entries, backfilled 2026-10-02, who classified, what the tiers mean (1). 4 Defect catalogue, one subsection per class (3). 5 The audit: protocol, readers, agreement, disagreements resolved (1.5). 6 Protocol and checklist, each mechanism with what it does not prove (1.5). 7 Threats to validity (1). 8 Discussion: negative results as an asset (0.5).

**Evidence from the logbook (entry ids; no number is printed that is not recounted by script from the entries).** Index state 2026-10-02: 15 closed rows, none tier A or B, nine with an INVALID-DESIGN label on at least one question, no REFUTED, TX the one null against expectation (recount at submission). **Negative-result framing.** The paper is itself a report of what did not work and why. If the blind audit shows low agreement, the paper says the taxonomy is not reliable across readers and narrows the catalogue to the classes with agreement; if the audit shows high agreement, the claim is reliability among LLM readers only. Both outcomes are printed in the same section with the same prominence, and the audit protocol is time-stamped before the readers run.

De-anonymize to the Zenodo DOI after notification.

Private material stays out: employer-identity commits, the retired human study, workshop recordings, client names. The UOC ethics committee is not reachable before enrolment.

**Why it supports GS even if reviewers are skeptical.** A skeptic can reject the novelty (guidelines exist) or the self-audit; neither removes the public record that the programme listed all of its evaluations, labelled nine as invalid, built countermeasures before running the next experiment, and measured whether outsiders classify them the same way. That record is the credibility the later papers (P1-P4, ROOT) draw on; a rejection with reviews also supplies an external critique of the checklist at no cost.
