## VIII. DISCUSSION AND IMPLICATIONS FOR PRACTICE

### A. What a practitioner should take from the evidence

The evidence supports a narrow practical reading. Authored structure of either kind (a detailed expert prompt or a GS cascade) beat unstructured use on structure and completeness, on every vendor tested, so the most defensible advice is not to run an agent on a bare specification. Where the generated artifact diverges from intent, the specification left a degree of freedom open, and naming the constraint (an acceptance criterion and a gate that fails when it is violated) closes it; the seven properties are a checklist for doing this systematically. Bounding the reader's context with a sentinel is cheap, and in the retrieval and twin studies the authored map was what lowered search cost. De-inflating duplicated surface lowered change cost on a frontier model, which a map cannot do. The generation-cost premium is real (about 2.9 times the naive run and 1.4 times the expert prompt per generation), so GS is not a cost-saving technique at generation time.

The irreducible human role is at the crossing from a situated intention to a specification: deciding what is worth building and what a stakeholder means as distinct from what they first asked. The discipline strengthens the crossing from specification to code and does not remove the person who ratifies that the specification captures the intention.

### B. What the expert-prompt tie does and does not test

An expert prompt is Generative Specification content delivered through the prompt. The control states the stack, the layering rule, the error contract, naming and a coverage target, which are the obligations a specification constitution states, without the persistent substrate (cascade, sentinel, enforcing gates, derivation lock, audit trail). The tie in Section VI.B therefore says that, for single-shot, short-horizon generation of a well-specified mid-complexity system by a capable model, the content matters on the measured metrics and the vehicle does not.

The study does not test four things. First, who knows what to put in the prompt: the control was written by the method's author. Second, whether quality persists across many iterations on a large project: each generation here is six or seven prompts for a system that fits in context, whereas agent code has been reported to degrade under iterative extension and quality guidance to reduce but not remove it [25]. Third, continuity across sessions and people: a prompt lives in a session and a substrate in the repository. Fourth, governance and auditability: no metric here measures whether a third party could ratify the result or reconstruct its rationale. A recent evaluation of repository-level context files reports no general gain in task success and higher cost [37], which is consistent in direction with our tie and cost premium and does not address these four questions either.

**Open hypothesis H-S.** Over a long horizon, a project that carries the persistent substrate shows less structural degradation and better preserved intent than the same project driven by an expert prompt containing the same obligations word for word. A fair test holds the obligations fixed and varies the increments one at a time (the prompt supplied in-session; the same text persisted as a file; plus enforcing gates; plus the derivation lock and a ratification record), runs many sequential feature iterations on a project too large for the context window with sessions and contributors rotating, and measures layer violations, duplication, complexity, mutation score on changed code, criteria coverage, regressions per iteration, and whether a newcomer can reconstruct earlier decisions. The prediction is no difference at the first iteration and a divergence that grows with iterations. It is refuted if the prompt arm tracks the substrate arm across the horizon, in which case the substrate adds nothing measurable on these axes and its value is confined to governance and continuity, which would need their own evidence. H-S has not been run. It is a hypothesis and not a result, and no conclusion of this paper depends on it.

## IX. CONCLUSION

AI-assisted code drifts because a stateless reader must infer what the author did not externalize. We presented Generative Specification as a discipline that makes derivability by such a reader the organizing criterion for a specification, with seven gradable properties. On one benchmark and one primary model, at five generations per condition, structured specification separates completely from unstructured use on architecture, completeness and test presence, and the separation holds across three vendors on static metrics; it does not separate significantly on the blind audit, and it ties a strong expert prompt on single-shot median quality. A capacity study on an invented benchmark found duplication and complexity differences pointing to a benefit largest on the weakest model and near zero at the frontier, inconclusive at three runs per cell, with behaviour and the layer metric uninformative. The boundaries are real: one author, one probably memorized benchmark, a metric the treatment targets, model-written tests, small samples, and a control that is GS without its substrate. The larger question, whether a persistent substrate beats a prompt-delivered specification over a long horizon, is stated as a falsifiable hypothesis and left for experiments that need an independent replicator and a second benchmark.
