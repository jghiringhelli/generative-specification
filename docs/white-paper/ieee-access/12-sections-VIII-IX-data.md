## VIII. DISCUSSION AND IMPLICATIONS FOR PRACTICE

The results reframe what a practitioner using an AI coding assistant should optimize. If code becomes near-free to regenerate, the scarce, non-regenerable good is the specification: the criterion for what correct means for this system. The practical consequences are concrete.

First, treat the specification, not the code, as the artifact under version control and review. A defect found in generated output is most usefully read as a query to the specification: what constraint, had it been written, would have made the defect unreachable. This turns debugging into specification refinement and makes each fix durable across regenerations, rather than a patch the next session may undo.

Second, specify prescriptively, not descriptively. The study's control condition shows that a strong prompt already yields type-clean, lint-clean code on this benchmark; where a generated artifact diverges from intent is where the specification left a degree of freedom open. Naming the constraint, an acceptance criterion the executor must satisfy and a gate that fails when it does not, closes that freedom. The seven properties are the checklist for doing this systematically, and two of them, Self-describing and Bounded, carry the most weight because a bounded, self-describing specification activates the model's relevant prior knowledge rather than the average of every similar project it has seen.

Third, bound the reader's context deliberately. A single unstructured specification loaded whole reproduces at the session level the very degradation it should prevent. A sentinel navigational tree, with tool-sequencing made explicit, routes each task to the slice it needs. In practice this is the most frequently missing piece and the cheapest to add.

The honest boundary is the human's. The study locates the irreducible human role at the crossing from a situated intention to a specification: deciding what is worth building, what the domain actually requires, and what a stakeholder means as distinct from what they first asked. The discipline automates and strengthens the crossing from specification to code; it does not remove the human who ratifies that the specification captures the intention. GS raises the ceiling of what a practitioner can build alone toward how completely they can describe what they want, and asks, in return, for the discipline to describe it.

## VIII.B. What the expert-prompt tie does and does not test

An expert prompt is Generative Specification content delivered through the prompt. The control in the study states the stack, the layering rule, the error contract, the naming conventions and a coverage target, which are the obligations a specification constitution states, without the persistent substrate (the artifact cascade, the sentinel, the enforcing gates, the derivation lock, the audit trail). The tie reported in Section VI.B therefore says that, for a single-shot, short-horizon generation of a well-specified mid-complexity system by a capable model, the content matters on the measured metrics and the vehicle that carries it does not.

The study does not test four things. First, who knows what to put in the prompt: the control prompt was written by the method's author, and whether a practitioner without that knowledge writes an equivalent prompt is not tested. Second, whether quality persists across many iterations on a large project: each generation here is six or seven prompts for a system that fits in context, whereas agent code has been reported to degrade under iterative extension [SlopCodeBench] and a prompt-level intervention was reported to improve initial quality without halting the degradation. Third, continuity across sessions and people: a prompt lives in a session and a substrate lives in the repository. Fourth, governance and auditability: none of the metrics measures whether a third party could ratify the result or reconstruct the reasons for it.

**Open hypothesis H-S.** Over a long horizon, a project that carries the persistent substrate shows less structural degradation and better preserved intent than the same project driven by an expert prompt containing the same obligations word for word. A fair test holds the obligations fixed and varies the increments one at a time (the prompt supplied in-session; the same text persisted as a file; plus enforcing gates; plus the derivation lock and a ratification record), runs many sequential feature iterations on a project too large for the context window with sessions and contributors rotating, and measures layer violations, duplication, complexity, mutation score on changed code, criteria coverage, regressions per iteration, and whether a newcomer can reconstruct earlier decisions. The prediction is no difference at the first iteration and a divergence that grows with iterations. The hypothesis is refuted if the prompt arm tracks the substrate arm across the horizon, in which case the substrate adds nothing measurable on these axes and its value is confined to governance and continuity, which would need their own evidence. H-S has not been run. It is a hypothesis and not a result, and no conclusion of this paper depends on it.

## IX. CONCLUSION

We have argued that AI-assisted code degrades not because models write poorly but because a stateless reader must infer everything the author did not externalize, and we have presented Generative Specification as a discipline that makes derivability by such a reader a binding constraint. The contribution is a named, measurable set of seven specification properties, a mechanism, the bridge and its read-asymmetry, that explains why externalizing intent in human-conceptual terms is effective, and a pre-registered evaluation on objective, rubric-independent metrics comparing unstructured use, expert prompting, and GS. The evidence indicates that structured specification separates completely from unstructured use on architecture, artifact completeness and test presence for this benchmark and model, does not separate significantly on the blind audit, lint or vulnerabilities, and ties a strong expert prompt on single-shot median quality; whether a persistent substrate outperforms a prompt-delivered specification over a long horizon is an open hypothesis (Section VIII.B). Observed gaps were specifiable and recoverable in an author-iterated series. The study's boundaries are real and stated: a single benchmark, a single primary model, a single author, and a replication of five runs per condition. The larger claim is modest and, we believe, durable: as the cost of producing code falls, the scarce skill becomes the criterion for specifying correctly, and that criterion is the part no quantity of generation replaces. Future work extends the evaluation across benchmarks and model families and formalizes the lifecycle-facing and internal partition of the properties.
