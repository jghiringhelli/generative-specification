# VII. THREATS TO VALIDITY (v0.3)

> Four categories after Wohlin et al. [47], with one paragraph each stating the threat and its mitigation, or that nothing mitigates it. LLM-specific obligations follow [49]. The five threats the reviewers will look for first are in bold: single author, a probably memorized benchmark, a treatment-targeted metric, the expert-prompt tie, and model-written tests.

## VII. THREATS TO VALIDITY

**Construct validity.** No single metric measures code quality. **The layer-violation metric counts `prisma.*` calls in route files, which is the rule that the control and treatment prompts state; it is targeted by the treatment and favors both structured conditions over the naive one by construction.** Mitigation is partial: the completeness metrics are not rule-specific, and the cross-vendor and capacity studies report duplication and complexity, which the prompts do not name; but no metric the treatment does not target (mutation score, a held-out behavioural oracle) was obtained for all three conditions, and that is owed. **The tests that GS and the other conditions generate are model-written, and the one mutation progression reported (58.6% to 93.1% on one services layer) was produced by model-written tests repaired until the score rose; it shows that mutation testing detects weak tests, not that GS caused better ones.** The seven-property audit is the author's rubric applied by two runs of one prompt by a model in the generator's family; its kappa measures run-to-run consistency and the artifacts reveal the condition. AI-reported coverage was not credible in the original run (93.1% claimed, 27.6% measured), so only executed figures are used. The naive condition lacked the emit rule imposed on the others.

**Internal validity.** **Single author, single lab:** the author designed the method, wrote the specifications, the rubric and the audit prompts, ran every experiment and scored it; there is no independent party and no human participant. Mitigations are partial: committed runners and raw outputs, an automated metric set, and reporting of nulls. The original registration is author-attested (the commits are absent from the public history) and the replication's plan cannot be shown to precede its runs. The post-hoc conditions were designed with knowledge of earlier gaps and are one run each. **The expert-prompt control is GS content delivered without the substrate and was written by the method's author, so the tie with it bounds the claim to single-shot, short-horizon generation and says nothing about who writes the prompt, long-horizon persistence, cross-session continuity or auditability (Section VIII.B).** BX applies the rubric to three implementations that GS never guided, which at n = 3 is a feasibility check on guidance circularity and not a closure of it.

**External validity.** **Conduit is probably memorized:** the RealWorld backend has many public implementations [46] that the models very likely saw, so measured quality may overstate what the same conditions achieve on an unseen domain, and the original supplement says so. The invented-domain CR study addresses this only partly (k = 3, structure only, no contamination canary result on file). The primary model is one Claude model; AX2 adds two vendors on static metrics inside one harness; the single-shot experiments and the twin studies use one benchmark. Whether results hold for other stacks, larger systems or human teams is not shown. The planned mitigations are a second non-memorized benchmark with the full protocol, a powered cross-model run on a controlled substrate, and an independent replicator.

**Conclusion validity.** At five against five the smallest exact p is 0.008, so deltas of 1.0 mean no overlap in five draws; Holm correction is within each metric and not across the metric family; the 0 of 5 versus 2 of 5 architecture-failure difference is not statistically supported (Holm p = 0.89). Single-run results are examples and not rates, and the 3/14 to 14/14 series is diagnostic. KX's ground truth derives from the structure the routed condition reads, so its claim is bounded to structural queries. Null results are reported in the same voice as positive ones (the tie with the expert prompt on audit score, files and layer violations; no significant difference on CVEs, type errors or lint). The token-reduction figures sometimes quoted in the field are not asserted: nothing here measures end-to-end session cost.
